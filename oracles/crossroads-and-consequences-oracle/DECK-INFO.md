# Crossroads and Consequences Oracle

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** Crossroads and Consequences Oracle  
**Deck family:** Oracle  
**Status:** In development — deck specification and complete card list only; no artwork or individual card files yet  
**Planned structure:** 48 numbered cards with no suits, organized around options, reversibility, cost, risk, responsibility, shared choice, delayed effects, and the consequences of acting, waiting, refusing, or remaining silent  
**Primary reading style:** image-led decision divination that helps compare available paths and trade-offs without pretending that a card can make a moral, legal, medical, financial, or life-defining decision on the querent's behalf.

## Core concept

Crossroads and Consequences Oracle treats every meaningful choice as an interaction between **values, information, cost, uncertainty, other people's agency, and what becomes possible afterward**.

Some choices are reversible experiments. Others create commitments that cannot be undone, even if later repaired. Some apparent choices are false either-or structures; some decisions have already been made through silence, avoidance, or continued participation. Sometimes another person controls the next move. Sometimes the most responsible action is to wait for information, and sometimes waiting itself carries the greatest cost.

The deck's central idea is:

> **Every path preserves something, costs something, and creates the conditions of the next choice.**

Each card can answer several decision questions:

- **options:** what paths are genuinely available rather than merely imagined;
- **agency:** who can choose, who must consent, and who bears consequence;
- **reversibility:** whether the choice can be tested, revised, repaired, or not easily undone;
- **cost:** what time, money, identity, relationship, security, or opportunity each path requires;
- **risk:** what is uncertain, what can be protected, and what cannot be guaranteed;
- **time horizon:** whether a path favors immediate relief or long-term benefit;
- **consequence:** what the current decision tends to set in motion;
- **values:** whether the choice is being made from fear, desire, habit, clarity, pressure, or responsibility.

The deck should clarify decisions, not replace informed consent, professional advice, practical research, or accountability.

## Visual style

The planned artwork uses **branching-path magical realism**: roads, doors, bridges, scales, tokens, maps, contracts, branching trees, rivers, and consequence landscapes painted in layered gouache with visible cause-and-effect threads.

- **Multiple paths:** choices are shown as genuinely different routes rather than cosmetic variations of one predetermined fate.
- **Visible consequences:** foreground actions connect through luminous lines to later terrain, structures, people, or costs in the distance.
- **Doors and thresholds:** entry, commitment, exclusion, opportunity, and irreversible transition become visually legible.
- **Weights and exchanges:** coins, stones, seeds, tools, keys, luggage, and empty hands show what each path asks or returns.
- **Time layers:** one image may show immediate relief in the foreground and delayed consequence farther away.
- **Other people at the crossroads:** shared decisions, consent, family pressure, institutional control, and competing needs are visible rather than erased.
- **Maps with incomplete areas:** research and foresight matter, but uncertainty remains part of choice.
- **Number medallion at the top:** the formal card number is visible before the title is read.
- **Card title at the bottom:** titles remain clear and stable.
- **Consistent frame:** a dark rust-indigo frame with fine gold branching stitches gives the deck one identity.
- **Layered palette:** gold indicates viable possibility; green indicates sustainable growth; red indicates urgency or irreversible cost; blue indicates reflection and uncertainty; gray indicates constraint; black indicates a genuine unknown rather than automatic danger.

The style is intentionally original and is **not designed to imitate a named living artist**.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to reason through **choice architecture**, not to issue commands disguised as destiny.

When interpreting a card, use this order:

1. **Observe what is literally present.** Identify routes, doors, figures, exchanged objects, barriers, signs, time layers, losses, gains, and who controls access.
2. **Define the actual decision.** Clarify what is being chosen, what is not being chosen, and whether the question contains a false binary.
3. **Identify the decision-maker.** The querent cannot choose another person's feelings, consent, treatment, employment, or participation.
4. **Assess reversibility.** Distinguish trial, pause, negotiation, commitment, disclosure, departure, and irreversible consequence.
5. **Name costs and benefits.** Include emotional, practical, financial, ethical, social, and long-term effects rather than only the desired reward.
6. **Compare time horizons.** Immediate relief may create later restriction; short-term difficulty may preserve long-term freedom.
7. **Read silence as behavior.** Delay, avoidance, and continued participation can produce consequences even without a spoken decision.
8. **Check information quality.** Some cards advise action with sufficient evidence; others advise research, professional input, or waiting.
9. **Preserve values and safety.** No card should pressure someone to ignore danger, consent, legal obligations, or professional boundaries.
10. **Synthesize rather than declare.** Explain what each card reveals about the choice and let the querent retain responsibility.

The documented meaning is a reference, not a rule that overrides real-world evidence, expert guidance, stated boundaries, or the consequences already visible.

## Numerology

The formal numbers `1` through `48` are part of the artwork and filenames.

- **1:** autonomous choice, initiation, one decisive route, or responsibility that cannot be delegated.
- **2:** two paths, polarity, negotiation, shared decision, or false binary.
- **3:** third option, social consequence, communication, coalition, or a decision affecting several parties.
- **4:** structure, contract, commitment, stability, or constrained options.
- **5:** risk, disruption, freedom, instability, or a choice forcing change.
- **6:** responsibility, care, compromise, mutual consequence, or values in relationship.
- **7:** deliberation, private choice, uncertainty, research, or strategic waiting.
- **8:** power, material consequence, obligation, exchange, or a decision with durable effects.
- **9:** completion, release, final consequence, compassionate ending, or a cycle ready to close.
- **0:** open possibility, amplification, consequence not yet fixed, or a choice space still forming.

Compound numbers may be read through both digits and reduction. Number symbolism does not convert a complex decision into a simple yes/no command.

## Decision language

The deck has no suits. Instead, cards operate across overlapping decision lenses.

### Option discovery

These cards identify open, familiar, unfamiliar, third, hidden, or falsely limited paths. They help distinguish actual options from wishful or coercive ones.

### Agency and consent

These cards clarify who chooses, when a decision is shared, when another person controls their own participation, and when silence or inaction is already shaping the outcome.

### Reversibility and commitment

These cards distinguish experiments, trial runs, negotiated pauses, points of commitment, and actions that close doors behind them.

### Cost, risk, and exchange

These cards show tolls, hidden costs, necessary loss, unexpected gain, safer risk, larger risk, compromise, and non-negotiable values.

### Time horizon

These cards compare immediate relief, short-term reward, delayed consequence, and long-term benefit.

### Relational consequence

These cards show bridges preserved or burned, burdens transferred, responsibilities accepted, and how one decision spreads through a family, team, or community.

### Decision quality

These cards ask whether choice comes from fear, desire, habit, clarity, pressure, or incomplete information.

## Recurring deck motifs

- **Crossroads:** genuine alternatives, ambiguity, agency, and the point where continued movement becomes choice.
- **Door:** opportunity, exclusion, commitment, consent, and what becomes inaccessible after entry or departure.
- **Bridge:** preserved relationship, negotiation, repair, shared route, or a connection deliberately burned.
- **Scale:** trade-off, fairness, cost, consequence, and comparison between unlike values.
- **Coin or token:** price, resource, opportunity cost, and what must be exchanged.
- **Seed:** long-term consequence, delayed benefit, and a small choice producing later growth.
- **Cage:** security, restriction, comfort, and the cost of preserving certainty.
- **Open hand:** release, trust, non-possession, offer, and accepting uncertainty.
- **Map:** research, planning, incomplete information, and the difference between imagined and available routes.
- **Footprints:** prior choices, accumulated consequence, habit, and evidence of where a path actually leads.
- **Thread between figures:** shared decision, dependence, transferred burden, and consequence spreading across people.
- **Burned landscape:** irreversible damage, completed rupture, or a route that cannot be restored exactly as before.
- **Open horizon:** freedom, uncertainty, and consequences that create further choices rather than one final answer.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported but should not be interpreted as a mechanical instruction to choose the opposite path.

A reversal can indicate:

- indecision, avoidance, or a choice being made unconsciously;
- a false option becoming visible;
- a previously irreversible decision becoming repairable;
- a reversible choice being treated as permanent;
- hidden cost, denied consequence, or transferred responsibility;
- excessive risk avoidance or reckless urgency;
- a compromise failing to meet anyone's essential needs;
- recovery after a damaging decision;
- a choice returning for reconsideration;
- or the need to pause until information improves.

The question, actual image, surrounding cards, practical facts, and who holds agency determine the most plausible expression.

- `CARDS.md` — the authoritative planned 48-card sequence and short descriptions.

No artwork or individual card-specific Markdown files exist yet.

When production begins, each card should have a numbered `.jpg` and matching `.md`, for example:

- `1-the-choice-arrives.jpg`
- `1-the-choice-arrives.md`

Each future card Markdown file must follow the repository's detailed standard, including literal visual inventory, core and shadow meanings, symbol analysis, decision lenses, available options, reversibility, costs, consequences, intentions, actions, advice, outcome, timing, spread interactions, examples, cautions, and a one-sentence essence.

Oracle-specific metadata should include at least **Deck**, **Formal number**, **Primary polarity**, **Primary decision lenses**, **Core theme**, and **Artwork status**.

Until final artwork is inspected, each visual description is an authoritative generation target rather than a claim about completed imagery.

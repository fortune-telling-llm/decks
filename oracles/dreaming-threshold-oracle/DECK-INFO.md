# The Dreaming Threshold Oracle

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** The Dreaming Threshold Oracle  
**Deck family:** Oracle  
**Status:** In development — deck specification and complete 54-card list established; no individual card files or final artwork yet  
**Planned structure:** 54 numbered cards with no suits, arranged as a continuous exploration of the deck's subject rather than a fixed predictive hierarchy  
**Primary reading style:** image-led exploration of dreams, recurring dream structures, memory, emotion, imagination, symbolic processing, sleep transitions, and the boundary between dream experience and waking life.

## Core concept

The Dreaming Threshold Oracle is built around one central principle:

> **The dream opens a threshold; meaning emerges through image, feeling, context, and waking integration.**

Dreams are threshold experiences where memory, feeling, body state, imagination, culture, and unresolved concerns recombine. The deck supports interpretation without treating every dream as prophecy, diagnosis, or supernatural visitation.

The deck describes current patterns, pressures, possibilities, and choices. It does not turn symbolism into proof, guarantee an outcome, replace direct evidence, or remove human agency. A card may identify a literal dream structure, an internal experience, a remembered relationship, a waking-life concern, or several of these at once.

## Visual style

The planned artwork uses a coherent visual language of **nocturnal architectural surrealism: impossible houses, flooded rooms, stations, forests, mirrors, staircases, animals, moons, beds, keys, doors, and shifting landscapes painted as soft gouache, graphite, translucent fabric, and silver thread**.

- **Strong central scene:** every card should communicate one readable dream event, figure, object, or environment at small size.
- **Layered secondary evidence:** gestures, object placement, distance, weather, wear, light, repeated counts, and background activity should materially change interpretation.
- **Visible transformation:** movement, repetition, altered scale, impossible physics, entry, escape, waking, and shifting identity should be shown rather than represented only by decoration.
- **Human variety:** figures should vary in age, body, skin tone, gender presentation, ability, role, and social context without assigning moral meaning to appearance.
- **Number medallion at the top:** the formal card number remains visible before the title is read.
- **Card title at the bottom:** each title is clear and stable.
- **Unified border:** a dark indigo or charcoal frame with fine silver stitching gives the deck a coherent nocturnal identity.
- **Original visual identity:** the style is original and is not designed to imitate a named living artist.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to read the **actual dream image, feeling, context, question, and spread**, not merely match the title to a fixed dictionary meaning.

When interpreting a card:

1. **Observe literally.** Identify people, roles, objects, setting, weather, light, color, number, distance, direction, body state, repetition, and visible transformation.
2. **Identify the lens.** Determine whether the position asks about setting, figure, movement, emotion, recent residue, recurring pattern, waking integration, or possible action.
3. **Separate symbol from proof.** A dream can be meaningful without establishing prophecy, another person's hidden thoughts, a diagnosis, or a supernatural event.
4. **Read the full range.** Consider constructive, frightening, excessive, blocked, inward, contextual, physiological, and recovery expressions.
5. **Check recent context.** Notice media, conversations, illness, medication, sleep disruption, trauma history, stress, and ordinary body sensations that may influence imagery.
6. **Look for cross-card patterns.** Repeated rooms, figures, animals, colors, directions, barriers, vehicles, water, and waking emotions should influence the synthesis.
7. **Use appropriate support.** Distressing, recurrent, trauma-linked, or sleep-disrupting experiences may deserve professional care rather than divinatory certainty.
8. **Synthesize.** Explain why the visible and remembered evidence supports the interpretation in this specific dream and spread.

The documented meaning is a strong reference, not a rule that overrides the final artwork, the dreamer's associations, or surrounding cards.

## Numerology

The formal numbers `1` through `54` are part of the deck sequence and future artwork.

- **1:** origin, initiation, focus, first image, or one active influence.
- **2:** polarity, comparison, dream and waking, self and double, or two possible positions.
- **3:** communication, development, social context, synthesis, or a third figure.
- **4:** room, structure, boundary, stability, system, or containment.
- **5:** movement, disruption, tension, adaptation, or changing conditions.
- **6:** relationship, responsibility, care, consequence, or restored balance.
- **7:** reflection, uncertainty, privacy, testing, or discernment.
- **8:** repetition, intensity, endurance, bodily sensation, or material effect.
- **9:** culmination, integration, release, wisdom, or the end of a dream cycle.
- **0:** openness, amplification, a cycle turning, or potential not yet fixed into one form.

Compound numbers may be read through both digits and, when useful, their reduction. Repeated formal numbers or clearly intentional counts inside the artwork can amplify a theme, but they remain supporting evidence rather than deterministic codes.

## Dream language

### Setting

Houses, rooms, roads, schools, stations, water, forests, cities, and other environments that organize the dreamer's emotional world.

### Figures

Known people, strangers, animals, the dead, doubles, children, pursuers, guides, and composite identities.

### Movement

Falling, flying, running, freezing, missing transport, returning, searching, crossing, and being unable to arrive.

### Transformation

Changing bodies, impossible objects, altered scale, repeating scenes, and symbols that shift meaning within the dream.

### Waking integration

Emotion on waking, recent experiences, body state, recurring patterns, practical reflection, and when professional support may be useful.

No card belongs exclusively to one lens. A literal dream object or setting may identify context, describe an emotion, represent recent residue, express a relationship, or symbolize an internal process. The dreamer's own associations and surrounding cards determine which use is most plausible.

## Recurring deck motifs

- **House and room:** self-structure, memory, privacy, family, unexplored material, or a current area of life.
- **Door, key, and threshold:** access, readiness, transition, boundary, curiosity, or withheld knowledge.
- **Water:** emotion, body state, overwhelm, cleansing, depth, memory, or change.
- **Train, road, and vehicle:** direction, timing, agency, missed opportunity, transition, or dependence on a larger system.
- **Animal:** instinct, fear, protection, desire, environment, cultural symbolism, or a remembered encounter.
- **Child or younger self:** vulnerability, development, memory, possibility, responsibility, or unmet need.
- **Dead or absent person:** grief, memory, continuing bond, unresolved feeling, or symbolic representation.
- **Mirror and double:** identity, projection, self-recognition, dissociation, comparison, or hidden perspective.
- **Falling and flying:** loss of control, release, freedom, arousal, fear, ambition, or altered bodily sensation during sleep.
- **Dawn and waking light:** integration, return, partial clarity, fading memory, and the transition back to ordinary reality.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported, but they should **not** be interpreted by mechanically negating the upright meaning.

A reversed card can indicate:

- blocked, delayed, inaccessible, or forgotten expression;
- an inward, private, hidden, or unspoken version of the card;
- excess, fear, depletion, distortion, obsession, or dysregulation;
- a dream image being interpreted too literally;
- recent residue or body state outweighing symbolic interpretation;
- recovery from a nightmare, repeated pattern, or difficult sleep period;
- release, integration, correction, or a symbol losing emotional power;
- or a need to inspect the scene from another figure's position.

The dream, artwork, spread position, waking context, surrounding cards, and real-world facts determine which expression is most plausible.

## File convention

The current deck folder contains:

- `DECK-INFO.md` — the deck identity, visual system, reading method, and future card-file standard;
- `CARDS.md` — the authoritative planned 54-card sequence and short descriptions.

No individual card artwork or card-specific Markdown files exist yet.

When card production begins, each completed card should be represented by:

- a numbered `.jpg` containing the artwork;
- a matching numbered `.md` containing a literal visual inventory plus detailed symbolic and divinatory interpretation.

Example:

- `1-the-recurring-dream.jpg`
- `1-the-recurring-dream.md`

Each future card Markdown file must follow the detailed structural standard used elsewhere in this repository and should contain:

- `# <number> — <card name>`
- `## Card metadata`
- `## Literal visual description`
- `## Core meaning`
- `### Upright keywords`
- `### Reversed / shadow keywords`
- `## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation`
- `## Divinatory interpretation by question type`
- `## Intentions` or the deck-appropriate equivalent
- `## Feelings` or internal-state interpretation where relevant
- `## Actions / likely next move`
- `## Advice`
- `## Outcome`
- `## Yes / no`
- `## Timing`
- `## Health / wellbeing readings`, kept non-diagnostic
- `## Spread interactions`
- `## Image-led reading examples`
- `## Fortune-teller cautions`
- `## One-sentence essence`

Until final artwork has been inspected, any future literal visual description is a generation target rather than a claim about a completed image. The text must be revised wherever the final artwork differs.

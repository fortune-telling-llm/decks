# Threads Between Us Oracle

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** Threads Between Us Oracle  
**Deck family:** Oracle  
**Status:** In development — deck specification and complete 60-card list established; detailed Markdown descriptions exist for cards 1–50  
**Planned structure:** 60 numbered cards with no suits, arranged as a continuous relational journey from recognition and meeting context through bonding, alignment, friction, boundaries, endings, and changed possibilities  
**Primary reading style:** image-led intuitive relationship divination that distinguishes who a person may be, how people may know each other, what form a bond is taking, how compatible its energies are, what purpose it may serve, and where the present pattern is likely to lead.

## Core concept

Threads Between Us Oracle treats every relationship as a **living thread between two lives**.

Some threads become romantic partnerships. Some become friendship, family, care, mentorship, work, collaboration, community, rivalry, healing, a brief crossing, or a necessary boundary. Some are strong but temporary. Some are meaningful precisely because they should not become closer. Some change form more than once.

The deck's central idea is:

> **A thread can be real without being romantic, permanent, mutual, or meant to be held forever.**

Each card is intentionally capable of answering more than one relational question. The same image may describe:

- **identity or context:** who the person is, the role they occupy, or how the people know each other;
- **bond form:** romance, friendship, family, work, care, mentorship, community, conflict, distance, or no substantial bond;
- **dynamic:** attraction, trust, projection, reciprocity, dependence, mismatch, secrecy, repair, or separation;
- **purpose:** comfort, growth, redirection, witness, teaching, healing, collaboration, boundary, closure, or awakening;
- **direction:** what the current pattern is tending toward if nobody changes it;
- **agency:** what choices, communication, consent, or boundaries could alter the pattern.

The cards describe **current energies, patterns, and possibilities**, not an unchangeable decree. Fate may create a meeting or a strong tendency, but people still make choices. “Meant to be” should therefore be read as **the strongest present purpose or potential**, not as permission to ignore reality, consent, safety, or another person's stated boundaries.

## Visual style

The planned artwork uses a consistent language of **woven magical realism**: intimate human environments and symbolic landscapes rendered like hand-painted gouache, illuminated textile collage, and fine threadwork.

- **Visible relational threads:** luminous cords, seams, braids, knots, loose ends, woven paths, and cut fibers make connection, tension, reciprocity, and separation visually readable.
- **Everyday meeting places:** homes, shared tables, offices, workshops, schools, clinics, waiting rooms, stations, gardens, streets, online screens, bridges, and shorelines allow a card to suggest how people may know each other.
- **Symbolic objects:** keys, doors, windows, mirrors, lanterns, letters, name badges, cups, chairs, anchors, compasses, scales, suitcases, and tools carry both literal and relational meanings.
- **People without rigid role stereotypes:** figures should vary in age, body, skin tone, presentation, ability, and relationship configuration. Gender presentation must not predetermine who is romantic, nurturing, authoritative, dependent, or powerful.
- **Strong central story:** every card should remain legible at small size while rewarding close inspection of gestures, distances, eye-lines, objects, thread direction, background activity, and negative space.
- **Number medallion at the top:** the formal card number remains visible before the title is read.
- **Card title at the bottom:** each title is clear, stable, and readable.
- **Textile border:** a dark indigo or charcoal frame with fine copper-gold stitching gives the deck one coherent visual identity.
- **Layered palette:** rose and ember tones can suggest attraction; blue and silver can suggest trust, distance, or reflection; green can suggest care and growth; gold can suggest choice, value, or purpose; gray and black can suggest limits, absence, or the unknown. These are tendencies, not fixed one-color keywords.
- **Meaningful empty space:** an absent figure, unoccupied chair, uncrossed bridge, unopened door, or broken line can be as important as what is present.

The style is intentionally original and is **not designed to imitate a named living artist**.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to read the **actual picture and spread**, not merely match a title to a predetermined verdict.

When interpreting a card, use this order:

1. **Observe what is literally present.** Identify people, roles, clothing, objects, rooms, landscape, weather, light, colors, numbers, gestures, distances, barriers, openings, and the direction or condition of every visible thread.
2. **Identify the reading lens.** Determine whether the spread position asks about identity/context, feelings, intentions, relationship form, compatibility, purpose, likely action, direction, boundary, or advice.
3. **Separate context from bond form.** A workshop may identify a coworker, but it may instead describe a relationship that requires practical teamwork. A lantern may identify a caregiver or mentor, but it may instead describe one person guiding the other.
4. **Keep categories distinct.** Attraction is not commitment. Care is not automatically romance. Familiarity is not compatibility. A person's feelings, intentions, likely actions, and actual capacity are different questions.
5. **Assess mutuality and power.** Notice whether both figures participate, whether one carries the thread, whether access is equal, and whether professional, caregiving, age, status, dependency, or authority differences affect the interpretation.
6. **Read the card's full range.** Use the documented upright, shadow, reversed, contextual, and symbol-specific meanings rather than reducing the card to its title.
7. **Look for cross-card patterns.** Repeated threads, colors, rooms, paths, roles, barriers, numbers, directions, weather, and paired or absent figures should influence the reading.
8. **Preserve agency.** State what the spread presently favors, what could change it, and where direct communication or a clear boundary matters more than divination.
9. **Synthesize rather than keyword-match.** The final interpretation should explain why the visible symbols support a specific relational reading in this question and spread.

The documented meaning is a strong reference, not a rule that overrides the actual image or surrounding cards.

A reading is symbolic evidence, not proof of another person's private thoughts. It must never be used to override explicit communication, consent, professional ethics, safety concerns, or a clearly stated “no.”

## Numerology

The formal numbers `1` through `60` are part of the artwork, filenames, and deck sequence.

- **1:** beginning, selfhood, recognition, initiation, or one person's influence.
- **2:** reciprocity, polarity, choice between two, pairing, or imbalance between two sides.
- **3:** communication, triangulation, social context, creativity, or a third influence.
- **4:** structure, stability, home, role, boundary, or containment.
- **5:** change, tension, movement, friction, or disruption.
- **6:** care, relationship, responsibility, harmony, repair, or the choices required to sustain connection.
- **7:** privacy, reflection, trust, uncertainty, discernment, or inner knowledge.
- **8:** exchange, power, endurance, repetition, obligation, or interdependence.
- **9:** culmination, compassion, wisdom, release, or the end of a relational lesson.
- **0:** an open field, amplification, a cycle turning, or potential not yet fixed into one form.

The complete count of **60** foregrounds `6` as the deck's relational and caregiving current, while `0` preserves openness and change. This is a supporting design choice, not a claim that every relationship has one numerically predetermined fate.

Compound numbers may be read through both digits and, when useful, their reduction. Repeated numbers in a spread can amplify a theme. Counts of secondary objects may also matter when visually clear and intentional, but they remain supporting signals rather than replacing the card's formal number.

## Relational language

The deck has no suits. Instead, every card can operate across several overlapping relational lenses.

### Recognition and context

This lens asks **who the person may be or how the people know each other**: family, friend, coworker, classmate, caregiver, mentor, client, professional, neighbor, online contact, traveler, ex, stranger, or someone met through community or crisis.

### Bond form

This lens asks **what the connection is trying to become**: romance, friendship, family-like belonging, collaboration, mentorship, care, a temporary crossing, a private bond, distance, or no meaningful relationship.

### Energy and compatibility

This lens reads chemistry, ease, trust, rhythm, attraction, reciprocity, similarity, difference, projection, friction, mismatch, power, and emotional capacity.

### Purpose and lesson

This lens asks why the connection matters: to awaken, comfort, teach, redirect, challenge, witness, heal, create, repair, protect, reveal a pattern, establish a boundary, or complete a cycle.

### Movement and timing

This lens reads whether the bond is beginning, growing slowly, returning, circling, pausing, remaining parallel, changing form, becoming stable, or moving toward separation.

### Boundaries and endings

This lens identifies professional limits, unequal access, rejection, secrecy, depletion, harmful entanglement, necessary distance, grief, closure, and the freedom created by a completed thread.

No card belongs exclusively to one lens. For example, **The Workshop** may identify a coworker or describe a bond built through shared labor; **The Lantern Bearer** may identify a caregiver or describe guidance; **The Dissonant Chord** may describe incompatible energies even when the people are family, coworkers, friends, or romantically attracted.

## Recurring deck motifs

These motifs may recur across cards. Their meaning must still be adapted to the specific image and question.

- **Single thread:** one active line of connection, one person's effort, a fragile beginning, or a specific bond isolated from the wider network.
- **Two joined threads:** reciprocity, pairing, mutual recognition, exchange, or two lives intentionally meeting.
- **Braids and woven cloth:** durable interdependence, shared history, many experiences forming one bond, or difficulty separating lives cleanly.
- **Knots and tangles:** commitment, complication, mixed roles, dependency, obligation, triangles, or a problem requiring patient untangling.
- **Fraying, loose ends, and shears:** neglect, incompletion, weakening contact, separation, release, or a thread that has fulfilled its purpose.
- **Doors, gates, locks, and keys:** access, consent, readiness, compatibility, privacy, opportunity, rejection, and the difference between invitation and intrusion.
- **Windows and mirrors:** observation, projection, self-recognition, visibility without access, honest reflection, or a barrier that remains transparent.
- **Lanterns and stars:** guidance, care, witness, orientation, hope, and a person who changes the direction of another life.
- **Roads, tracks, bridges, and shorelines:** shared direction, parallel lives, crossing, distance, mediation, relocation, timing, and whether two paths can genuinely meet.
- **Tables, hearths, workshops, schools, and waiting rooms:** family, community, work, learning, care, institutional roles, and bonds formed through repeated context.
- **Cups, chairs, and empty spaces:** emotional availability, reciprocity, absence, grief, waiting, or the shape left by someone who cannot be present.
- **Gardens, seeds, roots, and changing seasons:** growth, patience, family inheritance, life stage, fertility, care, decay, renewal, and timing.
- **Weather and tides:** emotional climate, crisis, recurring contact, pressure, return, cleansing, and forces larger than either person's immediate control.
- **Hands and body orientation:** agency, giving, receiving, pursuit, withdrawal, protection, dominance, equality, and whether both people are participating.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported, but they should **not** be interpreted by mechanically negating the upright meaning.

A reversed card can indicate:

- blocked, delayed, or unavailable expression;
- an inward, hidden, private, or unspoken version of the card;
- one-sided participation or unequal access;
- excess, dependency, enmeshment, or dysregulation;
- role confusion or a professional/personal boundary becoming unclear;
- misidentifying the relationship's actual form;
- recovery from the card's difficult expression;
- release, de-escalation, or a bond changing into a healthier form;
- or a need to inspect the image from the other person's position.

A reversed context card does not simply mean “this person is not a coworker,” “not family,” or “not a caregiver.” It may show that the role is ending, hidden, distorted, resented, exceeded, or no longer the most important part of the relationship.

The question, spread position, artwork, surrounding cards, and real-world facts determine which expression is most plausible.

- `## Spread interactions`
- `## Image-led reading examples`
- `## Fortune-teller cautions`

Oracle-specific card metadata should include at least:

- **Deck**
- **Formal number**
- **Primary polarity**
- **Primary relational lenses**
- **Core theme**
- **Artwork status**

Until a final image has been inspected, each card's literal visual description is an authoritative generation target rather than a claim about completed artwork. After any image is finalized, every visual description and interpretation must be checked and revised wherever the artwork differs.

The card Markdown must be detailed enough that a model can reason about the artwork when direct image access is unavailable, while direct image inspection remains preferred whenever possible.

## Artwork provenance

Artwork is produced separately from the Markdown card-description work and may be added or revised independently on the branch.

Final artwork should be original imagery created specifically for Threads Between Us Oracle. The accompanying descriptions and interpretations are written for this repository and must evolve together with the artwork so that every visual claim remains synchronized with the actual card.

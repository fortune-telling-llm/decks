# The Many Faces Oracle

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** The Many Faces Oracle  
**Deck family:** Oracle  
**Status:** In development — deck specification and complete card list only; no artwork or individual card files yet  
**Planned structure:** 72 numbered cards with no suits, covering personal roles, relationship contexts, professions, family positions, social functions, recurring archetypes, and the possibility that the relevant “person” is an aspect of the querent  
**Primary reading style:** image-led intuitive identity and context divination that asks who a person may be, how they enter the situation, what function they presently serve, and whether the role is literal, symbolic, temporary, changing, or projected.

## Core concept

The Many Faces Oracle is built around the fact that people are encountered through **roles**, but no person is reducible to a role.

A card may identify someone literally—a coworker, sibling, teacher, therapist, traveler, client, or former lover. The same card may instead describe how a person is behaving, the setting in which the meeting occurs, the power relationship between people, or the purpose the connection serves. A teacher can be a classroom professional, a mentor, a difficult experience, or the person from whom the querent learns what not to repeat.

The deck's central idea is:

> **A face tells you who appears in the story; the surrounding cards reveal who they actually are within it.**

Each card can operate through several questions:

- **literal identity:** the person's job, family relation, social position, or practical role;
- **meeting context:** where or through whom the people are likely to know one another;
- **relational function:** what the person contributes, requests, protects, interrupts, or reflects;
- **power:** who has authority, access, dependence, influence, or responsibility;
- **duration:** whether the role is permanent, temporary, transitional, or already ending;
- **projection:** whether the querent is seeing the real person or assigning an expected part to them;
- **self-reference:** whether the card describes the querent, a future self, or an unclaimed capacity rather than an external person.

The deck should never be used to infer protected traits, diagnose a stranger, identify a real person from minimal evidence, or override what someone explicitly says about their identity, role, boundaries, or intentions.

## Visual style

The planned artwork uses **portrait-based theatrical magical realism**: richly painted figures placed in meaningful environments, with garments, tools, doors, masks, mirrors, badges, seating, and background scenes that reveal both literal and symbolic role information.

- **Central portrait with environmental evidence:** each figure remains readable at small size while the setting provides clues about context and function.
- **Roles shown through action, not stereotype:** a caregiver is shown offering appropriate support; an authority is shown controlling access; a collaborator is shown making something jointly.
- **Varied humanity:** figures should vary in age, body, skin tone, presentation, ability, culture, and relationship configuration without assigning moral or relational meaning to appearance.
- **Multiple-layer scenes:** windows, reflected spaces, or background figures may show the difference between public role and private person.
- **Tools and tokens:** keys, letters, books, medical objects, contracts, instruments, luggage, craft tools, screens, and family objects identify context without requiring uniforms.
- **Masks and removable garments:** role can be performed, temporary, protective, or imposed.
- **Seats and positions:** height, distance, circles, desks, thresholds, and shared tables reveal hierarchy and access.
- **Number medallion at the top:** the formal card number remains visible before the title is read.
- **Card title at the bottom:** each title is stable and readable.
- **Consistent frame:** a dark plum-indigo frame with fine antique-gold portrait stitching gives the deck one coherent identity.
- **Layered palette:** gold indicates recognized function or influence; blue indicates distance and professionalism; green indicates care and development; red indicates attraction, urgency, or rivalry; gray indicates anonymity, uncertainty, or role transition.

The style is intentionally original and is **not designed to imitate a named living artist**.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to distinguish **person, role, context, function, and projection** rather than collapsing them into one claim.

When interpreting a card, use this order:

1. **Observe what is literally present.** Identify the figure, action, tools, clothing, environment, other people, barriers, gestures, eye-lines, symbols, and signs of authority or dependence.
2. **Determine the reading lens.** Is the spread position asking who, where, how the people know each other, what role is being played, or what function the person serves?
3. **Test the literal reading first.** A coworker card may genuinely point to work, but it should not be forced when the image and question support collaboration more broadly.
4. **Separate role from character.** Being a supervisor, patient, parent, or public figure does not determine whether someone is kind, compatible, trustworthy, or available.
5. **Assess power and ethics.** Professional, caregiving, educational, financial, legal, age, and institutional roles can create real limits on intimacy and consent.
6. **Look for changing roles.** Reversals and surrounding cards may show promotion, departure, retirement, estrangement, role confusion, or a relationship moving from one category to another.
7. **Check for projection.** The querent may expect a rescuer, rival, lover, or authority where the real person occupies a different role.
8. **Allow self-reference.** A card can describe the querent's own behavior, emerging identity, or responsibility.
9. **Synthesize with other decks and cards.** This deck says who or what role appears; other cards should clarify compatibility, timing, intention, and outcome.
10. **State uncertainty honestly.** Offer plausible contexts rather than claiming to identify a specific person without evidence.

A reading is symbolic interpretation, not a background check, identity-verification system, or proof of another person's private thoughts.

## Numerology

The formal numbers `1` through `72` are part of the artwork and filenames.

- **1:** individuality, initiator, singular role, self-reference, or a person acting alone.
- **2:** pair, counterpart, helper, rival, reciprocity, or role defined through another person.
- **3:** social context, communication, triangle, introduction, teaching, or a third influence.
- **4:** established role, institution, family structure, authority, or stable responsibility.
- **5:** change of role, conflict, outsider, disruption, or transition.
- **6:** care, service, relationship, responsibility, mediation, or community function.
- **7:** private identity, specialist knowledge, observation, spiritual role, or uncertainty.
- **8:** work, power, exchange, hierarchy, dependence, or durable obligation.
- **9:** elder, witness, completion of a role, wisdom, release, or compassionate service.
- **0:** open identity, amplification, anonymous presence, or a role not yet fixed.

Compound numbers may be read through both digits and reduction. Formal numbering does not rank one role as more important or valuable than another.

## Role language

The deck has no suits. Instead, cards operate across overlapping role lenses.

### Familiarity and relationship history

These cards distinguish stranger, familiar face, new friend, old friend, former lover, returning person, family, and chosen family. Familiarity should not be confused with compatibility or entitlement.

### Learning and guidance

Teacher, student, mentor, apprentice, spiritual guide, and specialist cards describe instruction, expertise, developmental roles, and the power responsibilities that accompany them.

### Work and exchange

Coworker, supervisor, employee, client, collaborator, competitor, business partner, service provider, benefactor, and debtor cards identify practical contexts and resource relationships.

### Care and dependence

Caregiver, healer, therapist, medical professional, patient, protector, dependent, and rescuer cards show support, vulnerability, boundaries, and the risk of confusing care with possession or romance.

### Social and public roles

Leader, follower, public figure, performer, host, guest, community organizer, messenger, matchmaker, gatekeeper, and silent observer cards describe visibility, influence, access, and social positioning.

### Family and inherited roles

Sibling, parent, child, elder, in-law, chosen family, and ancestor's echo cards identify literal kinship or inherited relational patterns that shape the present.

### Archetypal function

Mirror, catalyst, instigator, mediator, witness, temporary companion, and the self-reflective final card describe what someone does within the story even when their literal identity is different.

## Recurring deck motifs

- **Face and gaze:** recognition, attention, avoidance, public identity, and how the person meets the viewer.
- **Mask:** privacy, performance, profession, protection, deception, or a role that can be removed.
- **Mirror:** projection, resemblance, self-reference, counterpart, and recognition through another.
- **Badge or token:** official capacity, membership, access, responsibility, or institutional definition.
- **Tool:** profession, skill, service, craft, and the practical function a person performs.
- **Door or threshold:** introduction, access, gatekeeping, arrival, departure, and role transition.
- **Chair or elevation:** status, authority, invitation, dependency, and who is permitted to speak or decide.
- **Shared table:** collaboration, family, service, negotiation, and community belonging.
- **Letter or message:** introduction, mediation, news, online contact, and a person acting as connector.
- **Garment:** social role, expectation, protection, identity expression, and a part that may be inherited or imposed.
- **Hand gesture:** offering, directing, teaching, receiving, withholding, protecting, or controlling.
- **Background group:** community, workplace, family system, audience, or the network through which the person is known.
- **Empty frame:** unknown identity, absent person, future role, or projection filling a gap in evidence.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported, but a reversed role card does not simply mean “not a teacher,” “not family,” or “not a coworker.”

A reversal can indicate:

- a role ending, changing, hidden, resented, or performed poorly;
- role confusion or boundary crossing;
- a private person behind a public function;
- an authority losing power;
- a dependent gaining autonomy;
- a helper becoming depleted;
- projection onto the wrong person;
- the querent refusing their own responsibility;
- recovery from an unhealthy role;
- or the archetypal function being more important than the literal job or relation.

The question, artwork, spread position, surrounding cards, and verified facts determine the most plausible expression.

## File convention

The current deck folder intentionally contains only:

- `DECK-INFO.md` — the deck identity, visual system, reading method, and future card-file standard;
- `CARDS.md` — the authoritative planned 72-card sequence and short descriptions.

No artwork or individual card-specific Markdown files exist yet.

When production begins, each card should have a numbered `.jpg` and matching `.md`, for example:

- `1-the-stranger.jpg`
- `1-the-stranger.md`

Each future card Markdown file must follow the repository's detailed standard, including literal visual inventory, core and shadow meaning, symbol analysis, literal-role and archetypal-role readings, how the people may know each other, power and boundary considerations, intentions, feelings, actions, advice, outcome, timing, spread interactions, examples, cautions, and a one-sentence essence.

Oracle-specific metadata should include at least **Deck**, **Formal number**, **Primary polarity**, **Primary role lenses**, **Core theme**, and **Artwork status**.

Until final artwork is inspected, each visual description is an authoritative generation target rather than a claim about completed imagery.

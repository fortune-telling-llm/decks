# The Inner House Oracle

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** The Inner House Oracle  
**Deck family:** Oracle  
**Status:** In development — deck specification and complete card list only; no artwork or individual card files yet  
**Planned structure:** 54 numbered cards with no suits, arranged as a symbolic house whose thresholds, rooms, infrastructure, objects, occupants, damage, repair, boundaries, and grounds represent parts of the inner life  
**Primary reading style:** image-led reflective divination for self-concept, emotional readiness, memory, attachment, boundaries, family influence, healing, identity, and the internal conditions through which relationships and choices are experienced.

## Core concept

The Inner House Oracle treats the psyche as a **home that is lived in, inherited, protected, neglected, repaired, expanded, and sometimes rebuilt**.

A room can represent a memory, role, need, relationship pattern, private self, creative capacity, defense, or area of life. Doors show access and consent. Windows show perception. Foundations show beliefs and material safety. Guests show influences that enter temporarily; residents show patterns that have become established. Damage is not proof that the whole house is ruined, and renovation is not the same as pretending nothing happened.

The deck's central idea is:

> **The way you inhabit your inner house shapes what you welcome, what you fear, what you protect, and what you are ready to build.**

Each card can answer several questions:

- **inner location:** what part of the self or life is active;
- **access:** what is open, private, hidden, avoided, or protected;
- **condition:** what is stable, neglected, damaged, crowded, empty, or under repair;
- **inheritance:** what beliefs, roles, memories, or objects came from family and earlier environments;
- **occupancy:** who or what has been given too much or too little inner space;
- **boundary:** what may enter, what must leave, and who holds the key;
- **readiness:** whether the internal structure can support intimacy, change, responsibility, rest, or expansion;
- **homecoming:** how the querent can inhabit themselves more consciously rather than living only through others' expectations.

The deck supports reflection and symbolic exploration. It does not diagnose mental illness, recover hidden memories as fact, or replace trauma-informed professional care.

## Visual style

The planned artwork uses **architectural cutaway magical realism**: richly painted houses, rooms, gardens, stairways, doors, furniture, foundations, weather, and human figures shown through dollhouse-like sections, layered perspective, and illuminated textile detail.

- **Cutaway interiors:** several rooms may be visible at once, showing how one inner state affects another.
- **Rooms as distinct emotional climates:** light, clutter, temperature, color, sound, and use reveal whether a space is safe, active, abandoned, inherited, or changing.
- **Doors, locks, and keys:** access, consent, privacy, avoidance, invitation, and readiness are central visual language.
- **Windows and mirrors:** perception, projection, exposure, hope, distorted self-image, and contact with the outer world.
- **Infrastructure:** foundations, roofs, pipes, walls, stairs, wiring, and exits show beliefs, protection, regulation, connection, and emergency capacity.
- **Objects with history:** portraits, inherited furniture, boxes, toys, letters, tools, dishes, bedding, and empty frames carry memory and role.
- **Gardens and boundaries:** yards, fences, gates, roots, overgrowth, and cultivated ground show how inner life meets community and relationship.
- **Human figures without fixed roles:** residents, guests, caretakers, children, elders, and absent figures remain diverse and symbolically flexible.
- **Number medallion at the top:** the formal card number remains visible before the title is read.
- **Card title at the bottom:** titles remain clear and readable.
- **Consistent frame:** a charcoal-indigo frame with copper-gold architectural stitching unifies the deck.
- **Layered palette:** warm amber indicates inhabited safety; green indicates growth; blue indicates privacy and reflection; red indicates activation or conflict; gray indicates vacancy, neglect, or uncertainty; gold indicates meaningful access and repair.

The style is intentionally original and is **not designed to imitate a named living artist**.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to read the **condition and use of symbolic inner space**, not to label the querent.

When interpreting a card, use this order:

1. **Observe what is literally present.** Identify room, architecture, occupants, objects, light, weather, damage, tools, access points, clutter, emptiness, and boundaries.
2. **Identify the house lens.** Is the card describing identity, memory, relationship readiness, family inheritance, body, work, creativity, safety, or rest?
3. **Separate room from whole house.** Difficulty in one area does not define the whole person; a beautiful room does not prove the entire structure is stable.
4. **Read access and consent.** Doors, keys, guests, locks, and windows show who may enter and what should remain private.
5. **Assess infrastructure.** Foundations, roofs, exits, walls, and utilities reveal support systems and practical capacity beneath visible decoration.
6. **Distinguish vacancy from possibility.** An empty room may mean grief, absence, rest, preparation, or available space.
7. **Notice inheritance.** Ask which objects, rules, and arrangements were chosen and which were received from family, culture, or earlier survival.
8. **Read repair realistically.** Renovation requires time, tools, support, and sometimes professional help; symbolic insight is not the repair itself.
9. **Look for cross-card architecture.** Repeated doors, rooms, damage, guests, foundations, and gardens can form one coherent map of the inner house.
10. **Return agency to the resident.** The querent decides what to keep, change, protect, invite, and release.

The documented meaning is a reference, not a rule that overrides lived experience, direct memory, professional support, or the querent's own boundaries.

## Numerology

The formal numbers `1` through `54` are part of the artwork and filenames.

- **1:** self, first room, personal agency, single resident, or an inner state seeking recognition.
- **2:** relationship, shared room, mirror, threshold between two spaces, or divided need.
- **3:** communication, family triangle, creative room, social space, or movement between levels.
- **4:** foundation, wall, structure, home, containment, and established rule.
- **5:** disruption, renovation, moving, conflict, damaged structure, or changed occupancy.
- **6:** care, domestic responsibility, belonging, repair, shared life, and emotional shelter.
- **7:** private room, attic, hidden staircase, reflection, solitude, and inner knowledge.
- **8:** material support, maintenance, power, ownership, dependence, and durable pattern.
- **9:** inherited memory, grief, completion, emptying, wisdom, and preparing to leave a room behind.
- **0:** open space, amplification, unknown room, potential, or a house whose final form is not fixed.

Compound numbers may be read through both digits and reduction. Number symbolism supports the image but should not be used to diagnose or rank inner states.

## House language

The deck has no suits. Instead, cards operate through overlapping architectural lenses.

### Thresholds and access

Front doors, back doors, garden doors, gates, keys, and entry halls describe invitation, privacy, consent, readiness, and the difference between looking in and being welcomed.

### Shared and relational rooms

Living rooms, kitchens, dining rooms, guest rooms, shared bedrooms, and nurseries show belonging, intimacy, family roles, hospitality, care, and unmet relational needs.

### Private and hidden rooms

Studies, closets, attics, basements, unnamed rooms, and soundproof spaces show solitude, memory, shame, creativity, secrecy, protection, and what has not yet been integrated.

### Infrastructure and safety

Foundations, roofs, walls, windows, wiring, escape routes, fences, and repairs show beliefs, boundaries, regulation, resilience, and practical support.

### Inheritance and memory

Childhood rooms, family portraits, inherited furniture, empty frames, house rules, and unpacked boxes reveal what was received, carried, repeated, or left unresolved.

### Occupants and influence

Residents, guests, caretakers, intruders, and absent people show whose needs, voices, expectations, or memories occupy inner space.

### Renovation and homecoming

Rooms under repair, new foundations, moving day, the house at rest, and the final home-within card describe conscious change, release, rebuilding, and self-belonging.

## Recurring deck motifs

- **Door:** consent, invitation, opportunity, avoidance, and access between inner states.
- **Key:** agency, trust, readiness, responsibility, and who controls entry.
- **Window:** perception, exposure, hope, projection, and contact with the outer world.
- **Mirror:** self-image, comparison, recognition, and distorted or clarified identity.
- **Foundation:** core belief, material safety, attachment base, and what supports the visible life.
- **Roof:** protection, containment, maintenance, and vulnerability to external pressure.
- **Staircase:** development, transition between conscious levels, effort, and hidden access.
- **Furniture:** inherited role, comfort, habit, memory, and arrangements that can be moved.
- **Box:** stored history, unfinished transition, resource, or identity not yet unpacked.
- **Portrait:** family story, internalized person, remembered role, and inherited expectation.
- **Empty chair or room:** absence, grief, availability, waiting, and space not yet claimed.
- **Garden:** cultivation, body, relationship to the world, creativity, and what grows beyond walls.
- **Fence or gate:** boundary, community, protection, exclusion, and negotiated contact.
- **Repair tools:** capacity, support, skill, practical healing, and work still required after insight.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported but should not be interpreted by declaring that a room simply does not exist.

A reversal can indicate:

- blocked or denied access;
- a private room becoming visible;
- overcrowding, vacancy, or misuse of space;
- inherited rules being questioned;
- repair delayed, rushed, or finally completed;
- a guest overstaying or an intruder leaving;
- a locked room opening safely;
- the querent abandoning themselves to maintain another person's comfort;
- a damaged structure being stabilized;
- or a need to interpret the house from another resident's perspective.

The question, artwork, spread position, surrounding cards, and lived reality determine the most plausible expression.

## File convention

The current deck folder intentionally contains only:

- `DECK-INFO.md` — the deck identity, visual system, reflective method, and future card-file standard;
- `CARDS.md` — the authoritative planned 54-card sequence and short descriptions.

No artwork or individual card-specific Markdown files exist yet.

When production begins, each card should have a numbered `.jpg` and matching `.md`, for example:

- `1-the-front-door.jpg`
- `1-the-front-door.md`

Each future card Markdown file must follow the repository's detailed standard, including literal visual inventory, core and shadow meanings, symbol analysis, self-concept and relationship readings, access and boundary considerations, intentions, feelings, actions, advice, outcome, timing, spread interactions, examples, cautions, and a one-sentence essence.

Oracle-specific metadata should include at least **Deck**, **Formal number**, **Primary polarity**, **Primary house lenses**, **Core theme**, and **Artwork status**.

Until final artwork is inspected, each visual description is an authoritative generation target rather than a claim about completed imagery.

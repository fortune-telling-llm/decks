# The Inner House Oracle — Cards

This file defines the planned **54-card** sequence for The Inner House Oracle.

Each card represents a symbolic threshold, room, structure, object, occupant, boundary, condition, repair, or transition within the inner life. A room may describe self-concept, memory, family influence, relationship readiness, privacy, creativity, grief, safety, or a role that has been given too much space.

The house is a reflective metaphor, not a diagnosis. One damaged room does not define the whole person, and an empty room can represent possibility as easily as loss.

These short descriptions are planning summaries, not substitutes for future image-led card files. No individual artwork or card-specific `.md` files are included yet.

| Number | Card name | Short description |
|---:|---|---|

| 3 | **The Entry Hall** | First impressions, orientation, social presentation, and the place where outside influence is received before deeper access. |
| 4 | **The Guest Room** | Temporary closeness, hospitality, a role prepared for others, or inner space that belongs neither fully to self nor visitor. |
| 5 | **The Living Room** | Everyday belonging, shared identity, companionship, family atmosphere, and the part of self most available to trusted others. |
| 6 | **The Kitchen Table** | Nourishment, ordinary truth, domestic labor, care, conversation, and the relationships built through repeated daily exchange. |
| 7 | **The Pantry** | Stored emotional and material resources, scarcity memory, preparation, preservation, and what is available during difficulty. |
| 8 | **The Hearth** | Warmth, regulation, home, attachment, continuity, and the inner source around which people or parts gather. |
| 9 | **The Dining Room** | Ritual, family expectation, celebration, hospitality, hierarchy, and the rules governing who is included at the table. |
| 10 | **The Childhood Bedroom** | Early identity, attachment memory, old safety strategies, play, fear, and roles learned before adult choice. |
| 11 | **The Shared Bedroom** | Intimacy, vulnerability, sleep, sexuality, cohabitation, and the negotiations required when private life becomes relational. |
| 12 | **The Empty Nursery** | Unfulfilled possibility, grief, creativity not yet born, changed family expectations, or space waiting for a different purpose. |

| 18 | **The Locked Attic** | Inherited memory, forgotten material, family story, old beliefs, or knowledge kept above ordinary awareness. |
| 19 | **The Basement** | Foundation-level fear, survival memory, neglected need, practical infrastructure, and what supports the house from below. |

| 29 | **The Soundproof Room** | Protective silence, isolation, inability to be heard, deliberate retreat, or a part of self cut off from relational feedback. |
| 30 | **The Fire Escape** | Emergency boundary, backup plan, crisis resource, and the right to leave a situation that has become unsafe. |
| 31 | **The Back Door** | Private exit, informal access, familiar return, avoidance of confrontation, or a route used by people who know the house well. |
| 32 | **The Garden Door** | Movement from protected interior into growth, body, creativity, community, and the living world beyond thought. |
| 33 | **The Courtyard** | Protected openness, social contact within boundaries, shared air, and a meeting place between private rooms. |
| 34 | **The Secret Garden** | Cherished private growth, inner refuge, hidden relationship to beauty, or a living part needing protection rather than exposure. |

| 42 | **The Family Portraits** | Internalized relatives, inherited stories, belonging, loyalty, comparison, and the gaze of those who shaped the household. |
| 43 | **The Empty Frame** | Missing identity, absent person, unknown future, erased history, or space available for a story not yet chosen. |

| 50 | **The House at Rest** | Safety, quiet, recovery, ordinary regulation, and permission for the inner system to stop performing or defending. |
| 51 | **The House With No Walls** | Radical openness, poor boundaries, freedom, exposure, spiritual expansiveness, or identity not contained by former structures. |
| 52 | **The New Foundation** | Core beliefs, support, home, habits, or identity are being rebuilt to carry a different future. |
| 53 | **Moving Day** | A major identity, relationship, home, or life transition requires sorting what is carried, discarded, gifted, and grieved. |
| 54 | **The Home Within** | Belonging is becoming internally rooted; connection can be chosen without asking another person to provide the entire sense of home. |

# The Web of Influence Oracle

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** The Web of Influence Oracle  
**Deck family:** Oracle  
**Status:** In development — deck specification and complete 60-card list established; no individual card files or final artwork yet  
**Planned structure:** 60 numbered cards with no suits, arranged as a continuous exploration of the deck's subject rather than a fixed predictive hierarchy  
**Primary reading style:** image-led divination about groups, families, workplaces, communities, institutions, social pressure, alliances, hierarchy, reputation, mediation, and third-party influence.

## Core concept

The Web of Influence Oracle is built around one central principle:

> **The visible bond is one thread inside a larger web of loyalties, pressures, witnesses, systems, and choices.**

No relationship exists entirely alone. Families, friends, coworkers, audiences, institutions, cultures, algorithms, laws, and shared histories shape what people can see, say, choose, and risk. The deck maps influence without assuming every third party is hostile or secretly controlling.

The deck describes current patterns, pressures, possibilities, and choices. It does not turn symbolism into proof, guarantee an outcome, replace direct evidence, or remove human agency. A card may identify a literal person or group, an institutional rule, a reputation dynamic, a hidden dependency, a collective pattern, or several of these at once.

## Visual style

The planned artwork uses a coherent visual language of **social cartography rendered as woven magical realism: circles of figures, branching threads, tables, councils, family walls, office floors, public squares, stages, message chains, gates, towers, and networks painted in gouache and textile collage with colored lines showing influence**.

- **Strong central scene:** every card should communicate one readable social event, role, structure, or environment at small size.
- **Layered secondary evidence:** gestures, seating, distance, eye-lines, access, clothing, badges, messages, repeated counts, and background activity should materially change interpretation.
- **Visible influence:** alliance, pressure, information flow, exclusion, mediation, hierarchy, and redistribution should be shown rather than represented only by decoration.
- **Human variety:** figures should vary in age, body, skin tone, gender presentation, ability, role, and social context without assigning moral meaning to appearance.
- **Number medallion at the top:** the formal card number remains visible before the title is read.
- **Card title at the bottom:** each title is clear and stable.
- **Unified border:** a dark charcoal, indigo, or deep earth-toned frame with fine metallic stitching or network lines keeps the deck visually coherent.
- **Original visual identity:** the style is original and is not designed to imitate a named living artist.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to read the **actual picture, question, and spread**, not merely match the title to a fixed keyword.

When interpreting a card:

1. **Observe literally.** Identify people, roles, groups, objects, setting, seating, access, light, color, number, distance, direction, messages, and visible action.
2. **Identify the lens.** Determine whether the position asks about membership, authority, information, reputation, alliance, conflict, collective responsibility, or change.
3. **Separate influence from proof.** A card can identify a plausible social pressure without proving conspiracy, private coordination, another person's thoughts, or a guaranteed outcome.
4. **Read the full range.** Consider constructive, difficult, excessive, blocked, inward, formal, informal, contextual, and recovery expressions.
5. **Check power and representation.** Notice who can decide, who bears risk, who controls resources or information, who is absent, and whose voice is not being heard.
6. **Look for cross-card patterns.** Repeated circles, badges, tables, gates, stages, records, threads, positions, and directions should influence the synthesis.
7. **Use real-world evidence.** Direct communication, documentation, policy, consent, safeguarding, professional advice, and observable behavior outrank divinatory speculation.
8. **Synthesize.** Explain why the visible evidence supports the interpretation in this specific spread.

The documented meaning is a strong reference, not a rule that overrides the final artwork or surrounding cards.

## Numerology

The formal numbers `1` through `60` are part of the deck sequence and future artwork.

- **1:** origin, initiation, focus, leadership, or one dominant influence.
- **2:** polarity, alliance, rivalry, exchange, comparison, or two positions.
- **3:** communication, coalition, triangulation, development, or a third influence.
- **4:** structure, boundary, hierarchy, institution, or containment.
- **5:** movement, disruption, conflict, adaptation, or changing membership.
- **6:** relationship, responsibility, care, consequence, or restored balance.
- **7:** reflection, privacy, secrecy, uncertainty, testing, or discernment.
- **8:** power, exchange, repetition, endurance, access, or systemic effect.
- **9:** culmination, integration, release, accountability, or the end of a group cycle.
- **0:** openness, amplification, a network turning, or potential not yet fixed into one form.

Compound numbers may be read through both digits and, when useful, their reduction. Repeated formal numbers or clearly intentional counts inside the artwork can amplify a theme, but they remain supporting evidence rather than deterministic codes.

## Influence language

### Membership and belonging

Who is inside, outside, invited, tolerated, dependent, central, or peripheral within a group.

### Power and hierarchy

Formal authority, informal status, gatekeeping, expertise, dependency, institutional rules, and control of resources.

### Information and reputation

Gossip, secrecy, public image, testimony, algorithms, rumor, documentation, and who controls the story.

### Alliance and conflict

Support, rivalry, mediation, coalition, scapegoating, favoritism, shared enemies, and shifting loyalties.

### Collective change

Group decisions, accountability, reform, departure, new membership, cultural shifts, and the web reorganizing after one thread moves.

No card belongs exclusively to one lens. A literal person, group, object, or setting may identify context, describe influence, reveal a power structure, or symbolize an internalized social role. The question and surrounding cards determine which use is most plausible.

## Recurring deck motifs

- **Woven web:** interdependence, communication, distributed influence, entanglement, or collective resilience.
- **Inner and outer circles:** belonging, access, status, intimacy, exclusion, or layers of trust.
- **Table or council:** decision-making, negotiation, representation, leadership, or whose voice is heard.
- **Gate and badge:** membership, credentials, institutional access, role, permission, or gatekeeping.
- **Whispering thread:** gossip, rumor, informal communication, distortion, or information traveling without accountability.
- **Stage and audience:** public image, performance, reputation, attention, social pressure, or visibility.
- **Crown and empty chair:** authority, succession, absent leadership, responsibility, or symbolic power.
- **Bridge figure:** mediator, translator, connector, introducer, or person carrying information between groups.
- **Broken strand:** departure, exclusion, boundary, conflict, reform, or a network adapting to loss.
- **Many hands:** collective labor, mutual aid, shared responsibility, diffuse accountability, or pressure to conform.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported, but they should **not** be interpreted by mechanically negating the upright meaning.

A reversed card can indicate:

- blocked, delayed, inaccessible, or unavailable influence;
- an inward, private, hidden, or unspoken version of the card;
- excess, depletion, distortion, obsession, or social dysregulation;
- unequal participation, misplaced responsibility, or confused roles;
- formal authority without real influence, or informal influence without accountability;
- recovery from exclusion, conflict, rumor, or a harmful group pattern;
- release, de-escalation, reform, or a network losing power;
- or a need to inspect the scene from another member's position.

The question, artwork, spread position, surrounding cards, and real-world facts determine which expression is most plausible.

## File convention

The current deck folder contains:

- `DECK-INFO.md` — the deck identity, visual system, reading method, and future card-file standard;
- `CARDS.md` — the authoritative planned 60-card sequence and short descriptions.

No individual card artwork or card-specific Markdown files exist yet.

When card production begins, each completed card should be represented by:

- a numbered `.jpg` containing the artwork;
- a matching numbered `.md` containing a literal visual inventory plus detailed symbolic and divinatory interpretation.

Example:

- `1-the-inner-circle.jpg`
- `1-the-inner-circle.md`

Each future card Markdown file must follow the detailed structural standard used elsewhere in this repository and should contain:

- `# <number> — <card name>`
- `## Card metadata`
- `## Literal visual description`
- `## Core meaning`
- `### Upright keywords`
- `### Reversed / shadow keywords`
- `## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation`
- `## Divinatory interpretation by question type`
- `## Intentions` or the deck-appropriate equivalent
- `## Feelings` or internal-state interpretation where relevant
- `## Actions / likely next move`
- `## Advice`
- `## Outcome`
- `## Yes / no`
- `## Timing`
- `## Health / wellbeing readings`, when relevant and kept non-diagnostic
- `## Spread interactions`
- `## Image-led reading examples`
- `## Fortune-teller cautions`
- `## One-sentence essence`

Until final artwork has been inspected, any future literal visual description is a generation target rather than a claim about a completed image. The text must be revised wherever the final artwork differs.

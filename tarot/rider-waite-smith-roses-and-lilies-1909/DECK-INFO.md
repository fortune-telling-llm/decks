# Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)  
**Deck family:** Tarot  
**Historical edition:** First William Rider & Son issue, London, December 1909, identified by the blue Roses & Lilies back design  
**Status:** Foundation documented and detailed Markdown descriptions completed for all 22 Major Arcana; the 56 Minor Arcana still await detailed card files; artwork is developed separately  
**Historical structure:** 78 cards: 22 fully illustrated Major Arcana and 56 fully illustrated Minor Arcana; Strength VIII and Justice XI  
**Primary reading style:** image-led reading of Pamela Colman Smith’s complete scenic sequence, supported by Arthur Edward Waite’s published structure and clearly identified later correspondences

## Core concept

The 1909 Rider–Waite–Smith deck joins Waite’s occult programme with Pamela Colman Smith’s narrative image-making. Its major innovation for later tarot practice is the fully illustrated Minor Arcana, which gives every pip card a human scene, setting, gesture, and dramatic relationship rather than only an arrangement of suit emblems.

This repository entry documents a particular historical deck or edition, not a generic modern tarot system. Printed titles, numbering, suit names, visual details, production history, and edition differences must be preserved rather than silently replaced with assumptions taken from later recolorings, redrawings, facsimiles, or derivative decks.

## Visual style

Strong black line, flat yet expressive color, medieval and Renaissance costume, theatrical staging, recurring mountains, water, gardens, architecture, celestial bodies, and figures whose posture and eye-line create readable narrative. The original Roses & Lilies edition is also edition-identifiable through its blue floral card back.

Every current and future card description must begin with literal inspection of the relevant historical image: figures, posture, direction, objects, heraldry, inscriptions, color blocks, number and placement of suit emblems, printing irregularities, damage, restoration choices, and any difference between surviving copies.

The completed Major Arcana files describe the intended 1909 image in detail while explicitly requiring synchronization against the exact scan eventually selected for the repository. They do not treat a later recoloring or generic Rider–Waite–Smith reproduction as automatically interchangeable with the first issue.

## Divination philosophy

The deck may be read historically, visually, symbolically, comparatively, or divinationally, but those modes should not be confused.

1. **Observe the actual card.** Edition-specific visual evidence comes before memorized keywords.
2. **Preserve historical structure.** Do not renumber trumps, rename suits, alter Strength VIII or Justice XI, or import visual details from another edition without labeling the comparison.
3. **Distinguish fact from interpretation.** Production history, printed inscriptions, visible symbols, Waite’s published meanings, Golden Dawn correspondences, and modern psychological readings are different categories.
4. **Read contextually.** A card’s role changes with the question, position, neighboring cards, direction, repetition, and chosen historical or divinatory method.
5. **Treat later systems as optional overlays.** Golden Dawn, astrological, Kabbalistic, Etteilla, Marseille, and modern psychological correspondences should be named when used rather than presented as printed evidence.
6. **Preserve agency and evidence.** Tarot does not prove another person’s private thoughts, override consent, replace professional advice, diagnose a condition, or guarantee a future event.
7. **Use proportionate cautions.** Difficult imagery must not be sensationalized into literal death, disaster, abuse, illness, infidelity, or supernatural attack without real-world evidence.
8. **Synthesize rather than keyword-match.** Explain how the card’s visible and historical features support the reading.

## Numerology

The Fool is numbered `0`; the trumps then run `I–XXI`. Waite exchanges the Marseille positions of Strength and Justice, placing Strength at `VIII` and Justice at `XI`. Number symbolism is inseparable from the illustrated scene and suit.

Formal numbers should be read first in the deck’s own sequence. Reductions, repeated numbers, elemental counts, or later esoteric correspondences may be used as secondary methods only when the reader identifies the system being applied.

The repository sequence runs from `1` to `78` so filenames remain stable while each Major Arcana file separately records its historical printed number.

## Suit language

Wands express fire, will, enterprise, passion, sexuality, creativity, and directed vitality. Cups express water, emotion, relationship, imagination, memory, and receptivity. Swords express air, thought, truth, conflict, decision, analysis, and communication. Pentacles express earth, body, work, money, resources, health of routine, skill, and material consequence.

Court rank, scene, emblem count, gesture, landscape, and suit order are edition-specific evidence. Because Smith illustrates every pip card scenically, the future Minor Arcana files must inspect the full narrative rather than reduce the card to a number-and-element formula.

## Recurring deck motifs

Infinity signs, roses and lilies, mountains, paths, rivers, towers, gardens, dogs, birds, sphinxes, angels, crowns, hands emerging from clouds, yellow skies, sun and moon faces, red banners, wreaths, crossed keys, vessels, swords, and the four living beings recur as structural signals. Smith’s staging often makes direction, distance, missing participation, posture, eye-line, and the contrast between foreground and horizon materially important.

Repetition across a spread—gestures, crowns, animals, celestial forms, architectural openings, vegetation, weapons, vessels, colors, directions, or suit-emblem arrangements—should influence interpretation when it is genuinely visible in this edition.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are optional. Historical packs were not always designed around the modern practice of mechanically assigning an opposite meaning to an inverted card.

When reversals are used, they may indicate blocked, delayed, inward, excessive, diminished, private, unstable, recovering, or differently oriented expression. The card’s actual inverted composition, Waite’s documented reversed range where relevant, the selected reading system, and surrounding cards should determine the interpretation.

A reversal should not be treated as automatic catastrophe, moral failure, or simple negation.

## Historical source notes

The target is the first 1909 Roses & Lilies issue rather than an unspecified later recoloring. Historical verification should distinguish original Smith linework and early Rider color from later redrawings, recolorings, border changes, cropping, and modern facsimiles. Waite and Smith should both be credited; the art should not be described as Waite’s artwork.

The Major Arcana files retain Waite’s historically documented meanings where useful but do not permit those brief lists to override Smith’s literal staging. Apparent contradictions between Waite’s upright and reversed lists are acknowledged rather than silently normalized into a modern keyword system.

Dates and titles for historical decks are sometimes approximate or normalized across catalogues. Repository text should identify uncertainty rather than converting scholarly convention into false precision.

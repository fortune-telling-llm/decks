# 16 — XV — The Devil

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Major Arcana
- **Repository sequence number:** 16
- **Historical card number:** XV
- **Printed title:** `THE DEVIL.`
- **Primary polarity:** binding, appetitive, materializing, obscuring
- **Primary esoteric correspondence:** Capricorn and the Hebrew letter Ayin in the Golden Dawn/Waite–Smith system; neither attribution is printed on the card
- **Core theme:** appetite, fear, shame, dependency, and unequal power become binding when they are treated as inevitable, hidden from awareness, or exchanged for the surrender of agency
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded image must be checked against an early-edition scan for exact gesture, chains, tails, symbols, color, linework, cropping, restoration, and print wear.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

The card is vertically oriented with the Roman numeral **`XV`** at the top and **`THE DEVIL.`** in the lower title panel. A large horned, winged, partly human and partly animal figure crouches on a dark pedestal. An inverted pentagram appears on or above the forehead. One hand is raised while the other holds a burning torch angled downward.

Below, a nude man and nude woman stand on opposite sides of the pedestal. Each wears a chain around the neck, and both chains attach to the central block. The chains appear loose enough that removal may be possible. Both figures have horns and animal-like tails: one tail ends in flame-like form, while the other is associated with grapes or fruit.

The composition echoes The Lovers but replaces the open landscape, angel, and sunlight with a dark enclosure, lowered light, pedestal, and chains.

### The central horned figure

The Devil’s body combines several forms.

- Large curved horns rise from the head.
- Bat-like wings spread behind the torso.
- The face has animal and human qualities.
- The lower body is furred or goat-like.
- The feet or claws grip the pedestal.
- The figure is elevated over the chained pair.

The hybrid body can symbolize instinct, taboo, fear, projection, sexuality, materiality, or a composite image onto which people place what they reject in themselves. It should not be used to equate bodies, sexuality, disability, animals, or nonconformity with evil.

### The inverted pentagram

A five-pointed star appears inverted at the forehead.

- One point faces downward.
- It occupies the area associated with sight and thought.
- Its inversion contrasts with more upright stars elsewhere in the deck.

The symbol can signify material or instinctive forces dominating awareness, a value system turned upside down, or perception organized around appetite, fear, and control. Specific occult doctrine should be identified as interpretation rather than literal text.

### The raised hand

One hand is lifted in a gesture that resembles blessing, command, or prohibition.

- The fingers are extended in a formal sign.
- The hand echoes the Hierophant’s public gesture while altering its tone.
- The central figure appears to claim authority over the pair.

This visual echo can indicate false sanction, charismatic control, authority that legitimizes bondage, or a system that presents submission as natural.

### The downward torch

The other hand holds a flaming torch angled toward the ground and toward the chained figures.

- Its light points downward rather than upward.
- The flame is close to the man’s fire-like tail.
- It can appear to ignite, stimulate, or intensify appetite.

The torch may symbolize intelligence used to inflame compulsion, sexuality, anger, consumption, or spectacle rather than illuminate choice. It can also show awareness turned toward the material and bodily realm—not inherently evil, but dangerous when divorced from consent and proportion.

### The chained man and woman

Two nude figures stand beneath the pedestal.

- Their bodies are exposed.
- They retain upright posture.
- Small horns suggest participation in or adaptation to the Devil’s field.
- Their hands are not visibly bound.
- The chains circle their necks and connect to the central structure.
- The loops look wider than the necks.

The figures are constrained, but not depicted as completely powerless. The loose chains make awareness and choice part of the card’s meaning while avoiding the cruel claim that all bondage is easily escaped. Addiction, poverty, coercive control, trauma, disability, debt, and systemic oppression can create real barriers that require support and protection—not blame.

### The tails

Each human figure has a tail.

- The man’s tail ends in a flame-like shape.
- The woman’s tail is associated with a cluster resembling grapes or fruit.
- The tails connect appetite and identity to the environment of bondage.

Flame can signify anger, sexuality, will, or escalating stimulation. Grapes can signify pleasure, intoxication, abundance, sensuality, or dependence on reward. Neither appetite is inherently wrong; the issue is whether it remains chosen and proportionate.

### The pedestal

The Devil crouches on a dark rectangular block.

- The block creates elevation and hierarchy.
- The chains attach to it rather than directly to the Devil’s body.
- It resembles an institution, altar, contract, platform, or fixed belief.

The pedestal suggests that bondage can be maintained by a structure: debt, workplace, family pattern, shame system, platform algorithm, market, ideology, or agreement. Removing one charismatic figure may not be enough if the pedestal remains.

### Darkness and restricted environment

The background is predominantly dark and lacks the expansive natural scenery seen in many other cards.

- There is little horizon.
- No clear path outward is shown.
- The central flame provides limited light.

Restricted perspective is part of bondage. When the immediate system appears to be the whole world, alternatives become difficult to imagine.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The dominant relationships are:

- **black and dark gray:** enclosure, fear, hidden structure, and reduced options;
- **red-orange flame:** appetite, arousal, anger, stimulation, and urgency;
- **pale human bodies:** exposure, vulnerability, and retained humanity;
- **animal features:** instinct, projection, adaptation, and taboo;
- **metal chains:** obligation, dependency, control, and visible connection.

The eye moves from the inverted pentagram to the raised hand and torch, down the chains, and across the two tails. Power, stimulation, and attachment form one system.

## Core meaning

The Devil represents **bondage maintained through compulsion, fear, shame, unequal power, material dependency, or the belief that no alternative exists**.

It can indicate addiction, obsession, coercive control, exploitative contract, debt, secrecy, sexual or material fixation, shame, manipulation, toxic workplace, abusive relationship, self-defeating habit, or appetite that has stopped being freely chosen.

The card also contains recoverable agency. The chains are visible and loose, and the figures retain hands, posture, and awareness. This does not mean escape is simple or that trapped people are responsible for their oppression. It means the reading should identify both the binding mechanism and the resources needed for safer choice.

Its healthy or integrative expression can involve naming desire honestly, reclaiming sexuality from shame, recognizing material needs, confronting dependency, obtaining treatment or support, renegotiating power, and refusing false spiritual purity.

Waite’s published divinatory range includes ravage, violence, vehemence, extraordinary efforts, force, fatality, and predestination not necessarily evil; his reversed range includes evil fatality, weakness, pettiness, and blindness. Those terms preserve the card’s concern with force and constrained perception but require careful modern handling.

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `XV` — relationship and choice under the pressure of appetite

The number `15` reduces to `6`, linking The Devil structurally with The Lovers. The same themes—desire, relationship, value, and choice—appear under conditions of diminished freedom.

Repeated sixes or fifteens can highlight attachment, responsibility, sexuality, family, or the difference between chosen bond and coerced bond.

### The Lovers echo — exposure without open sky

The nude pair recalls The Lovers, but the angel and sun are replaced by a dominant hybrid figure and torch.

This can indicate:

- attraction turned into dependency;
- values subordinated to appetite;
- relationship hidden from wider perspective;
- or shame distorting embodied desire.

### The loose chains — constrained but not erased agency

The chains may represent:

- debt;
- contract;
- addiction;
- family obligation;
- trauma bond;
- social stigma;
- economic dependence;
- or a belief that keeps the person attached.

Their looseness invites examination of options while requiring compassion for barriers.

### The pedestal — the system supporting bondage

The pedestal asks what structure makes the chain durable. It may be financial, institutional, technological, relational, ideological, or internalized.

### The torch — stimulation that prevents reflection

The downward flame can symbolize:

- intoxicating urgency;
- sexual arousal;
- outrage cycles;
- addictive media;
- escalating conflict;
- or attention captured by immediate reward.

### The tails — appetite incorporated into identity

When a habit or relationship becomes part of identity, change can feel like loss of self. The tails may show that adaptation to bondage is real and must be addressed without shame.

### The inverted star — perception organized downward

The inverted pentagram can indicate that awareness is serving appetite or material control rather than placing them within a wider value system.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible expressions include:

- intense sexual chemistry;
- obsession;
- secrecy;
- coercive control;
- jealousy;
- trauma bonding;
- dependency;
- an affair or triangle where supported by evidence;
- unequal financial or emotional power;
- or a relationship organized around shame and appetite.

The card does not prove abuse, infidelity, addiction, or evil intent. It signals a need to inspect consent, freedom, isolation, money, fear, and whether either person can safely say no or leave.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- intensely driven;
- charismatic;
- sexually expressive;
- materially focused;
- ashamed of desire;
- dependent on a substance, habit, status, person, or system;
- or occupying significant power over others.

Shadow possibilities include manipulation, coercion, obsession, exploitation, or denial. Do not diagnose addiction or personality disorder from tarot.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may contain:

- powerful attraction;
- mutual compulsion;
- secrecy;
- unequal access to resources;
- control through shame;
- dependency;
- or a shared pattern that narrows both people’s world.

The key questions are whether choice is free, the chains can be named, and outside support remains accessible.

### Career, projects, and study

The Devil can indicate:

- exploitative employment;
- golden handcuffs;
- burnout culture;
- manipulative leadership;
- addictive metrics;
- debt-funded obligation;
- unethical incentives;
- or a project sustained by fear and sunk costs.

It can also show intense ambition or material skill that needs ethical boundaries.

### Money and material questions

The card may indicate debt, predatory terms, compulsive spending, gambling, financial control, dependence on one payer, bribery, or profit tied to harm.

It is a strong cue to read contracts, seek qualified advice, and identify exit constraints. It is not proof that a particular investment or person is fraudulent.

### Creativity

The Devil can signify taboo material, erotic art, obsession with a project, powerful market appeal, or confronting shame through creation.

Its shadow is exploitation, compulsive work, audience capture, or sacrificing health and ethics to attention.

### Spirituality and personal growth

The card can support honest shadow work, reclaiming the body, naming taboo desires, and recognizing where fear or shame created false bondage.

It warns against coercive groups, charismatic authority, purity culture, spiritualized abuse, and teachings that claim suffering proves devotion.

## Intentions

As intentions, The Devil may indicate a desire to:

- possess;
- control;
- seduce;
- intensify;
- secure dependency;
- obtain material advantage;
- hide a bond;
- or satisfy appetite without considering long-term consequence.

It can also indicate an intention to confront or release a binding pattern, especially in supportive context.

## Feelings

As feelings, it can show:

- obsession;
- strong sexual attraction;
- jealousy;
- shame;
- fear of loss;
- dependency;
- resentment;
- craving;
- or the sense of being unable to disengage.

Intensity does not prove love or compatibility.

## Actions / likely next move

Likely actions include:

- pursuing gratification;
- reinforcing a dependency;
- applying pressure;
- hiding behavior;
- returning to a compulsive pattern;
- renegotiating a chain;
- seeking treatment or support;
- or testing whether the bond can survive greater freedom.

## Advice

The Devil advises:

> **Name the chain, the reward that maintains it, the structure that profits from it, and the support required for safer choice—without shaming the appetite or blaming the bound person.**

Where coercion, addiction, abuse, debt, or danger is real, seek qualified and practical support.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card can indicate:

- intensified attachment;
- dependency becoming visible;
- material success with a hidden cost;
- a binding agreement;
- confrontation with compulsion;
- or the beginning of release through honest recognition.

The outcome depends heavily on whether the chain is named and support is available.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: No for questions about freedom, safety, fairness, and healthy sustainability; yes for strong appetite, material intensity, obsession, or a binding arrangement.**

## Timing

The Devil can indicate:

- immediate recurrence when a trigger appears;
- a pattern that repeats until conditions change;
- a fifteen-unit period when numerology is intentionally used;
- or a Capricorn-linked period when that overlay is deliberately applied.

It often describes persistence rather than a single event.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, The Devil may symbolize addictive behavior, compulsive coping, shame, overwork, substance risk, sleep disruption, or dependence requiring compassionate professional support.

Do not diagnose addiction, advise abrupt withdrawal, or shame someone. Substance withdrawal and medication changes can be medically dangerous and require qualified care.

## Spread interactions

The Devil becomes especially significant with:

- **The Lovers:** chosen bond contrasted with constrained bond;
- **The Emperor:** authoritarian control or material power;
- **The Hierophant:** coercive institution, shame, or dogma;
- **Strength:** instinct and appetite requiring compassionate regulation;
- **Justice:** contract, debt, accountability, or legal scrutiny;
- **Death:** release from a binding form;
- **Temperance:** recovery, dosage, and rebalancing;
- **The Tower:** bondage exposed or broken through crisis;
- **The Star:** hope and replenishment after shame;
- **many Pentacles:** money, work, body, and dependency dominate;
- **many Cups:** emotional or substance dependence intensifies;
- **many Wands:** sexual, angry, or compulsive fire rises;
- **many Swords:** manipulation, fear, and mental loops require clarity.

Repeated chains, pedestals, horns, inverted stars, torches, grapes, flames, nude pairs, or dark enclosures can make dependency, appetite, shame, and power spread-level themes.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Why can’t I leave this relationship?”

The chains and pedestal direct attention to practical mechanisms: money, housing, fear, isolation, trauma, immigration status, children, shame, or intermittent reward. The answer is not “just take off the chain”; identify support and safety planning.

### Example: “Is this attraction healthy?”

The card confirms intensity but asks whether both people can choose freely, tolerate boundaries, remain connected to wider life, and distinguish desire from possession. Intensity without freedom is not enough.

### Example: “Why is this job so hard to quit?”

The pedestal may be status, income, health insurance, identity, or sunk cost. The torch may be urgency and addictive metrics. Build an exit plan that addresses the actual chain rather than relying on motivation alone.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not label a person evil, possessed, abusive, addicted, or unfaithful solely from this card.
- Do not shame sexuality, pleasure, material need, kink, or the body.
- Do not claim all chains are easily removable or blame people facing coercion and systemic barriers.
- Do not advise abrupt substance withdrawal or unsafe confrontation.
- Preserve the loose neck chains, pedestal, inverted pentagram, raised hand, downward torch, bat wings, horns, and different tails as visible details.
- Keep Capricorn/Ayin correspondence identified as an overlay, not printed evidence.
- Verify exact gestures and symbols against an actual 1909 Roses & Lilies scan.

## One-sentence essence

**Bondage grows where appetite, fear, and power narrow the world; freedom begins by naming the chain, restoring support and choice, and refusing shame as the price of embodiment.**

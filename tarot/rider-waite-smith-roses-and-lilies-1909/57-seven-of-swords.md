## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

A lone figure moves away from a cluster of tents while carrying five swords. Two additional swords remain standing upright in the ground behind. The person steps lightly or awkwardly across the foreground, body turned in the direction of departure while the head looks back toward the camp.

The carried swords are gathered by their blades rather than their hilts, forcing the figure into a careful, unusual grip. In the distance, several people appear near an open area among the tents. The sky is yellow, and the ground is pale and open.

The composition reads as **looking-back figure → five carried blades → two remaining blades → camp and distant people → open route away**.

### The central figure

The person occupies most of the foreground.

- The body leans forward in movement.
- The head turns back over the shoulder.
- The mouth may appear smiling, tense, or concentrated depending on the scan.
- The knees bend in a light-footed, tiptoeing, or hurried posture.
- The clothing includes warm red, yellow, blue, and patterned elements.
- No armor or visible companion appears.

The figure can represent a planner, scout, thief, defector, messenger, survivor, independent thinker, or person attempting to avoid direct confrontation. The image does not prove criminal intent.

### The five carried swords

Five blades are gathered against the figure’s body.

- Their hilts rise near the head and shoulder.
- Their points angle downward.
- The person appears to hold the sharp blades directly, requiring care.
- The collection is cumbersome and potentially self-injuring.
- The five swords constitute most, but not all, of the suit count.

These swords can symbolize information, ideas, evidence, tools, responsibilities, arguments, secrets, intellectual property, or resources being removed selectively. The awkward grip warns that a clever plan may carry hidden cost to the planner.

### The two swords left behind

Two swords remain planted in the ground near the camp.

- They stand separately from the five being carried.
- Their hilts remain accessible.
- They create a visible remainder or unresolved pair.
- The figure looks back toward them and the camp.

The two remaining swords can signify incomplete execution, unfinished business, witnesses, evidence left behind, a relationship or agreement not fully severed, or a deliberate choice not to take everything.

### The backward glance

The figure’s head turns opposite the direction of the feet.

- Attention remains attached to the place being left.
- The person may be checking whether they are observed.
- The glance can indicate caution, guilt, strategy, curiosity, or divided commitment.

This tension between movement and attention is central. Departure is occurring, but psychological separation is incomplete.

### The camp and tents

A group of peaked tents fills the middle ground.

- The tents suggest temporary settlement, military organization, event space, worksite, expedition, or community.
- Their patterned surfaces create social structure behind the solitary figure.
- The camp is not visibly destroyed.
- No owner of the swords is identified.

The camp can represent an organization, relationship system, family, team, institution, or temporary arrangement from which someone is extracting themselves or resources.

### The distant figures

Several small people appear near the camp.

- They are too distant for clear identities or expressions.
- One or more may gesture or engage in activity.
- Their awareness of the foreground figure is uncertain.

The distant people can signify witnesses, colleagues, opponents, distracted participants, or social context. Their small scale cautions against claiming that everyone is unaware or complicit.

### The open ground

The foreground is broad and lightly detailed.

- It provides space for escape or independent movement.
- No wall, river, or visible guard blocks the route.
- The figure’s main difficulty is the load and the need to remain unnoticed or unchallenged.

The open ground may symbolize opportunity, lack of oversight, freedom, or the exposure that comes with leaving a group structure.

### The yellow sky

The sky is bright rather than dark.

- The action occurs in visibility rather than night.
- There is no storm or supernatural concealment.
- What is strategic may still be observable.

The daylight complicates the common “secret theft” reading. The card can involve openly unconventional strategy, selective disclosure, or acting outside group expectations rather than literal hidden crime.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The principal relationships are:

- **yellow sky and ground:** visibility, thought, exposure, and mental activity;
- **red and patterned clothing:** intention, individuality, risk, and self-directed action;
- **silver swords:** information, leverage, plans, and sharp consequences;
- **colored tents:** group structure, temporary belonging, and social context.

The feet move one way, the head turns back, five swords travel, and two remain. Every major line emphasizes partial departure and divided attention.

## Core meaning

The Seven of Swords represents **strategy outside straightforward confrontation**.

It can describe secrecy, evasion, selective disclosure, independent action, planning, stealth, intellectual property, tactical withdrawal, avoiding unnecessary conflict, taking what is useful, leaving a group, or attempting something whose success depends on timing and incomplete visibility. The card is morally ambiguous because the image does not identify ownership, consent, or motive.

Waite describes a man rapidly carrying away five swords while two remain in the ground near a camp. His upright meanings include design, attempt, wish, hope, confidence, quarrelling, a plan that may fail, and annoyance; he explicitly states that the design is uncertain because the meanings diverge. Reversed, he lists good advice, counsel, instruction, slander, and babbling.

An image-led synthesis is:

> **A nonconventional plan may be necessary, but cleverness is not the same as ethics, completeness, or freedom from consequence.**

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Does this card prove my partner is cheating?”

No. It shows strategy, partial removal, and uncertain ownership. It can raise questions about disclosure and consent, but tarot is not evidence of an affair. Use observable facts and direct, safe communication.

### Example: “Should I tell my team about this prototype now?”

The card can support a protected development phase, but the awkward sword grip and two remaining blades warn against bypassing required security, ownership, or stakeholder obligations. Define what secrecy legitimately protects and when disclosure becomes necessary.

### Example: “Why can’t I fully move on?”

Five swords travel while two remain, and the head looks back. Part of the exit is real, while unfinished attachment, evidence, obligation, or narrative remains. Identify the two swords instead of calling the whole process failure.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not automatically translate the card into cheating, lying, theft, fraud, or betrayal.
- Do not identify the swords’ owner or the figure’s motive without evidence.
- Do not glorify secrecy when informed consent, safety, law, or professional ethics require disclosure.
- Do not shame privacy or strategic withdrawal when direct confrontation is unsafe.
- Preserve Waite’s explicit uncertainty about the card’s meaning.
- Keep Moon-in-Aquarius and later esoteric titles labeled as overlays, not printed evidence.
- Verify sword count, grip, tent colors, distant figures, numeral, crop, and body direction against the exact 1909 scan selected for the repository.

## One-sentence essence

**A clever exit carries five blades and leaves two behind: use strategy, but test every secret, shortcut, and omission against ethics, consent, and what remains unresolved.**

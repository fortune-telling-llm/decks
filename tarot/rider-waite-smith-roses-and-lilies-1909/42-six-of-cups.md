# 42 — Six of Cups

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Minor Arcana
- **Suit:** Cups
- **Element:** Water
- **Repository sequence number:** 42
- **Formal number:** 6
- **Printed inscription:** Roman numeral `VI` at the top; no printed card title appears on the face
- **Primary polarity:** familiar, generous, memory-bearing, gently reciprocal
- **Later esoteric correspondence:** Sun in Scorpio, traditionally titled Pleasure; these attributions are not printed on the card
- **Core theme:** affection, memory, and kindness move through a protected familiar setting, inviting the reader to distinguish living reciprocity from nostalgia, inherited role, and idealized return
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded scan must be checked for edition, cropping, color, restoration, wear, numeral form, and small print differences before the literal description is treated as synchronized.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

Two childlike or youthful figures stand in a stone courtyard surrounded by substantial buildings. The taller figure on the left bends toward the smaller figure on the right and offers a golden cup filled with a large white five-petaled flower.

Six golden cups appear across the scene. Five contain white flowers; one cup is being given. A large cup stands elevated on a stone pedestal at the left, while four more cups line the lower foreground. The architecture includes a tower, steep roof, walls, windows, and a distant adult or guard walking along the left background.

The Roman numeral **`VI`** appears at the top against a pale-blue sky.

The composition centers on **a gift passed between unequal-height figures inside a protected but structured precinct, with repeated cups of memory and care surrounding them**.

### The taller figure

The left figure bends forward while holding the flower-filled cup.

- A red-orange hood covers the head and shoulders.
- The upper body leans toward the smaller person.
- A blue skirt or tunic extends over red-orange leggings.
- The visible hand supports the cup at the smaller figure’s chest height.
- The face is mostly hidden by the hood and angle.
- The posture can be read as giving, presenting, teaching, remembering, or returning something familiar.

The difference in height may indicate age, role, power, experience, or simply perspective. The card should not automatically assign authority, innocence, or moral superiority based on size.

### The smaller figure

The right figure stands facing the giver.

- A red-orange hood or cap frames the head.
- A patterned or dotted outer garment covers the torso.
- Blue lower clothing and red footwear remain visible.
- One pale hand extends outward.
- The head tilts toward the cup and flower.

The smaller figure appears attentive and receptive. The image does not show force. Even so, any reading involving children, age differences, caregiving, teaching, or professional power must preserve real-world consent and safeguarding.

### The offered cup

The central cup is held between the two figures.

- It is golden and upright.
- A large white flower rises from it.
- The cup is offered rather than placed on the ground.
- It creates a direct line between giver and receiver.
- Its height is adjusted to the smaller figure rather than held above them.

The cup can symbolize kindness, memory, gift, apology, affection, teaching, tradition, or emotional material passed from one person or generation to another.

### The white flowers

White five-petaled flowers fill the cups.

- Their centers are dark or warm-toned.
- Green leaves surround the blossoms.
- The flowers are simple and repeated.
- They are cultivated within vessels rather than growing freely from the earth.

The flowers can represent innocence, sincerity, memory, gentleness, simple pleasure, ritualized affection, or a familiar emotional language. Because they are arranged in cups, the feeling may be preserved, offered, or repeated through established forms.

White flowers should not be used as proof that a past relationship or childhood was objectively pure or safe. Nostalgia can brighten selected memories while excluding harm.

### The four foreground cups

Four cups stand along the lower edge.

- Each contains a similar white flower.
- They create a repeated rhythm across the courtyard.
- They remain upright and available.
- Their arrangement resembles a stored collection of emotional memories or gifts.

The foreground cups may symbolize multiple past experiences, family customs, recurring kindnesses, childhood stages, or several resources inherited from prior relationships.

### The elevated cup and pedestal

A large flower-filled cup stands on a stone pedestal at the left.

- It is raised above the four foreground cups.
- The pedestal bears a shield-like emblem with a dark `X` or crossed form.
- The cup appears established as part of the courtyard.
- It may resemble a memorial, household object, ceremonial vessel, or protected emotional value.

The elevated cup can represent a family tradition, idealized memory, honored ancestor, protected innocence, or a story that has been given special status.

The `X` emblem should be recorded literally and not assigned one absolute heraldic meaning without a source.

### The courtyard

The ground is paved with pale stone.

- The scene is enclosed by buildings.
- The area appears orderly and protected.
- The figures are not in wilderness.
- The cups can stand securely on the surface.

The courtyard can symbolize home, childhood environment, institution, school, family estate, community, or a familiar emotional container. It offers safety and continuity while also suggesting inherited rules and limited horizons.

### The buildings and tower

Substantial architecture fills the background.

- A tall tower rises on the left.
- A steep roof and central gable occupy the middle.
- Windows and stone walls suggest habitation.
- The buildings are larger than the figures and visually enduring.

The architecture can signify family system, ancestry, tradition, class, institution, community memory, or structures that outlast individual relationships.

### The distant adult or guard

A small adult figure walks in the left background.

- The person carries or wears something dark.
- They appear separate from the children’s exchange.
- Their direction is away from the central pair.
- Their presence introduces supervision, work, security, or the wider adult world.

The distant figure may represent protection, authority, a caregiver, household worker, institutional context, or the adult realities that exist around childhood memory.

### The difference in scale

The giver is taller than the receiver, and the buildings dwarf both.

This layered scale can indicate:

- age difference;
- mentorship;
- older and younger self;
- generational exchange;
- power imbalance;
- or individual memory nested within a larger family or institutional system.

The card’s tenderness should not erase the need to examine power.

### The pale-blue sky

A clear pale-blue sky appears above the rooftops.

- No storm is present.
- The Roman numeral is highly visible.
- The calm atmosphere supports memory, familiarity, and gentle exchange.

The sky’s clarity may reflect idealization as much as objective safety. The reader should ask what is outside the frame.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The principal relationships are:

- **gold:** emotional value, memory, and the repeated vessels;
- **white:** simplicity, sincerity, memory, and idealized innocence;
- **red-orange:** warmth, vitality, family feeling, and embodied familiarity;
- **blue:** tenderness, reflection, continuity, and the Water suit;
- **green:** living affection and the ability of memory to continue growing;
- **pale stone:** structure, protection, tradition, and institutional containment.

The eye moves from the repeated foreground cups to the central offered flower, then upward through the two figures into the surrounding buildings. The visual sequence is:

> **stored memory → present gift → relationship → inherited structure**

## Core meaning

The Six of Cups represents **memory, familiarity, kindness, return, childhood, and emotional patterns carried from the past into the present**.

It can indicate reunion, an old friend, family, a former home, gifts, nostalgia, caregiving, younger people, older and younger versions of the self, or a new relationship that feels familiar because it echoes an established pattern.

Waite describes children in an old garden, their cups filled with flowers. His meanings include the past, memories, childhood, happiness or enjoyment derived from what has vanished, and—under another reading—new relations, new knowledge, and a new environment. Reversed, he gives the future, renewal, and what will happen presently.

The card asks:

> **What is returning: a living relationship, a useful value, an inherited role, or an idealized version of the past?**

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `6` — relationship, care, reciprocity, and repair

Six often concerns harmony, responsibility, relationship, and the work required to sustain care.

It may indicate:

- family;
- service;
- mutual aid;
- repair;
- beauty;
- or the balance between giving and receiving.

Repeated Sixes amplify relationship, responsibility, support, and the risk of caretaking becoming obligation.

### The gift — affection made concrete

The offered cup may symbolize:

- an apology;
- a present;
- a remembered kindness;
- teaching;
- care adapted to the recipient;
- or an emotional pattern passed on.

A gift is healthy when it can be accepted or declined without coercion or debt.

### The white flowers — preserved feeling

The flowers may indicate:

- innocence;
- sincerity;
- memory;
- ritual;
- family stories;
- or tenderness kept alive through repeated gestures.

Their placement in cups suggests emotion is cultivated and contained rather than entirely spontaneous.

### The older and younger figures — generational or inner exchange

The pair can represent:

- siblings;
- caregiver and child;
- older and younger self;
- mentor and learner;
- former and current identity;
- or two people with unequal experience.

The image should never be used to romanticize inappropriate age or authority differences.

### The courtyard — familiar container

The courtyard may signify:

- home;
- school;
- institution;
- family system;
- ancestral place;
- or a psychologically protected memory.

It can be secure, restrictive, or both.

### The distant guard — safety and authority

The background figure may indicate:

- supervision;
- protection;
- professional duty;
- family authority;
- or an adult world partially absent from the emotional exchange.

Their distance asks whether appropriate oversight, accountability, or support is genuinely present.

### Six cups — many memories, one present exchange

Five cups are stored or placed, while one is actively exchanged.

This can mean:

- history becoming relevant now;
- one memory selected from many;
- an old pattern being repeated or revised;
- or the present carrying a whole archive of prior relationships.

## Advice

The Six of Cups advises:

> **Receive the value of the past without surrendering the present to it; let kindness return, but examine the roles, power, and expectations carried inside the gift.**

Honor memory. Verify current reality. Give freely enough that the recipient remains free.

**Leaning: Yes for return, reunion, kindness, familiarity, and revisiting the past; conditional for romance or long-term compatibility.**

The answer may be “yes, this returns,” without meaning “yes, it should resume in the same form.”

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, the card may symbolize supportive memories, family or community care, reconnecting with safe play, working with a qualified therapist on childhood experiences, or receiving practical help.

It does not prove childhood trauma, developmental conditions, pregnancy, fertility, or any diagnosis. Real concerns require appropriate professional care and safeguarding.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Why does this new person feel so familiar?”

The repeated cups and old courtyard suggest that the connection activates existing emotional memory. That may be genuine shared resonance, but it may also be a family or attachment pattern. Learn who the person is now rather than letting familiarity write the whole story.

### Example: “Should I reconnect with my ex?”

The card supports contact with the past and remembered tenderness, but the surrounding architecture shows established patterns. Ask what has changed, whether the former role remains appropriate, and whether both people are free to choose a new form rather than repeat the old one.

### Example: “What gift can I offer this person?”

The giver lowers the cup to the recipient’s level. Offer something suited to their actual needs, not a gift designed to recreate your own memory or make them indebted.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not equate nostalgia with objective truth about the past.
- Do not romanticize age, authority, caregiving, therapeutic, educational, or family power differences.
- Do not use child figures to predict pregnancy or literal children without context.
- Do not assume an ex, estranged relative, or old friend will return simply because the card appears.
- Do not label all familiarity as a past-life connection; ordinary memory and pattern recognition are sufficient explanations.
- Do not treat gifts as automatically benevolent if they create obligation or dependence.
- Keep later Sun-in-Scorpio and Pleasure correspondences identified as overlays, not printed facts.
- Verify numeral, exact cup count, flower form, clothing, distant figure, shield emblem, buildings, and crop against the selected 1909 Roses & Lilies scan.

## One-sentence essence

**The past offers a flower to the present: receive the kindness, examine the inherited role, and let memory inform rather than govern what happens now.**

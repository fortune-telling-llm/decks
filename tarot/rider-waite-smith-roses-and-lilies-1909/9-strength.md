# 9 — VIII — Strength

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Major Arcana
- **Repository sequence number:** 9
- **Historical card number:** VIII
- **Printed title:** `STRENGTH.`
- **Primary polarity:** courageous, integrating, compassionate, regulating
- **Primary esoteric correspondence:** Leo and the Hebrew letter Teth in the Golden Dawn/Waite–Smith system; neither attribution is printed on the card
- **Core theme:** durable power arises when instinct and intensity are met with presence, courage, relationship, and proportion rather than humiliation or brute suppression
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded image must be checked against the early edition for color, linework, cropping, restoration, and print variation.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

The card is vertically oriented with the Roman numeral **`VIII`** at the top and **`STRENGTH.`** in the lower title panel. A woman in a pale robe bends calmly over a large lion in a green landscape. Her hands hold or guide the lion’s jaws. Above her head floats a horizontal infinity sign.

A long garland of flowers encircles her waist and extends around or between the figures. A second floral wreath rests on her head. The lion’s red tongue and mane create the strongest warm color in the lower image. A blue mountain rises in the distance beneath a yellow sky.

The scene contains no weapon, armor, cage, or visible struggle. Human and animal bodies are close, and the woman’s posture is gentle but fully engaged.

### The woman

The central human figure leans toward the lion with composure.

- She wears a long white or pale robe.
- Her head is crowned with flowers rather than metal.
- Her face is directed toward the animal.
- Her stance remains stable despite the lion’s size.
- Her arms are extended and actively in contact with the jaws.
- Her expression and posture do not suggest panic or cruelty.

Her strength is relational. She does not dominate from a distance; she enters the animal’s immediate space and remains present to its power.

### The lion

The lion occupies much of the lower foreground.

- Its mane is large and textured.
- Its body is powerful and grounded.
- Its tail curves visibly.
- Its mouth is open enough for the woman’s hands to guide it.
- The red tongue is visible.
- The animal appears engaged with the woman rather than attacking another target.

The lion can signify instinct, appetite, anger, sexuality, vitality, pride, fear, courage, or the body’s raw life force. The image does not show these forces being destroyed. They are being contacted, regulated, and incorporated.

### The hands at the lion’s jaws

The woman’s hands touch the lion’s mouth.

- One hand appears above and the other below or beside the jaws.
- The gesture can be read as opening, closing, calming, or guiding.
- The ambiguity matters: the image does not clearly depict violent force.

The jaws are where appetite, voice, bite, and breath become action. The hands may therefore symbolize regulating expression, meeting anger before it harms, helping instinct speak without letting it consume the whole situation, or using touch and trust where force would escalate resistance.

### The infinity sign

A horizontal figure-eight floats above the woman’s head.

- It echoes the Magician’s infinity symbol.
- It is centered over a scene of embodied relationship rather than a table of tools.
- It suggests continuity, renewable power, and the circulation between conscious attention and instinct.

Where the Magician directs external resources, Strength directs or befriends internal force.

### The floral garland

A long chain of flowers surrounds the woman’s waist and extends through the composition.

- It links human and animal visually.
- It is flexible rather than rigid.
- It may resemble a leash, but it is made of living flowers rather than rope or metal.
- The flowers echo the wreath on her head.

The garland can symbolize affection, relationship, cultivated instinct, voluntary bond, beauty as a form of regulation, or the continuity between thought, body, and environment. In shadow, a beautiful bond can still conceal control, so the actual posture and context must be examined.

### The white robe

The pale robe creates a field of calm around the woman.

- It contrasts with the lion’s mane and red tongue.
- It moves softly rather than forming armor.
- It suggests clarity, sincerity, restraint, or a nonaggressive form of courage.

White should not be equated with weakness or moral purity by default. Here it primarily emphasizes that her method does not imitate the lion’s force.

### The distant mountain

A blue mountain rises in the background.

- It is separated from the figures by open green land.
- Its peak echoes other aspirational mountains throughout the deck.
- It provides distance and future perspective.

The mountain can represent a long practice, an objective reached through self-regulation, or the enduring work required after one successful moment of courage.

### Yellow sky and green field

The sky is bright and exposed, while the ground is alive and green.

- The encounter occurs in daylight.
- There is room around the figures.
- The landscape is not a cage.

This suggests that regulation is occurring openly and voluntarily rather than through hidden confinement.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The dominant relationships are:

- **white:** calm method, clarity, and nonaggressive presence;
- **red tongue and warm mane:** appetite, anger, vitality, sexuality, and expressive force;
- **green:** life, body, healing, and grounded relationship;
- **yellow:** visibility, confidence, and conscious attention;
- **blue mountain:** long-term mastery, distance, and perspective.

The eye moves from the infinity sign to the woman’s face and hands, then into the lion’s open mouth and around the floral garland. The card makes contact—not conquest—the center of strength.

## Core meaning

Strength represents **courage that can remain in relationship with powerful instinct without becoming either its victim or its tyrant**.

It can signify resilience, compassion, patience, emotional regulation, confidence, vitality, sexuality, anger held proportionately, recovery, steady influence, and the capacity to meet fear directly. The card often appears when force is available but a gentler, more integrated method will produce a more durable result.

Its healthy expression does not require suppressing anger, desire, grief, or bodily need. It asks those forces to become conscious and directed. Its shadow includes domination disguised as care, repressed rage, performative calm, shame about instinct, compulsive appetite, weak boundaries, or exhaustion from continually containing what should be addressed structurally.

Waite’s divinatory list calls the card Fortitude and includes power, energy, action, courage, magnanimity, success, and honors; the reversed range includes despotism, abuse of power, weakness, discord, and disgrace. Those meanings align with the image’s contrast between compassionate power and force misused.

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `VIII` — power, exchange, endurance, and consequence

The number `8` can signify sustained power, reciprocity, repetition, material consequence, and the ability to hold a process over time.

Repeated eights may amplify endurance, control, work, power dynamics, or the need to examine who carries the ongoing load.

### The woman and lion — conscious and instinctive life

The pair can symbolize:

- mind and body;
- social self and appetite;
- compassion and anger;
- human relationship with animal nature;
- or two people with very different kinds of power.

The card does not require assigning the woman to “mind” and lion to “lower nature” in a rigid hierarchy. Both are living and necessary.

### The open jaws — expression at the threshold of action

The mouth is where need becomes sound or bite.

It can indicate:

- anger needing words rather than harm;
- desire needing consent and direction;
- appetite needing proportion;
- or a truth that must be expressed without destroying the relationship.

### The hands — skillful contact

Hands signify agency. Here they work at the point of greatest risk.

They may show:

- de-escalation;
- somatic regulation;
- caregiving;
- training;
- direct boundary-setting;
- or the courage to remain present while guiding intensity.

### The infinity sign — renewable inner power

The symbol suggests that strength can circulate rather than be spent in one act of force.

In shadow, endless self-control may become a loop that prevents necessary change or help-seeking.

### The floral bond — relationship instead of chain

Flowers indicate cultivated life, affection, and flexibility. The garland can suggest cooperation, trust, or influence accepted rather than imposed.

It also asks whether beauty and kindness are being used to conceal an unequal power dynamic.

### The mountain — mastery as a long practice

The distant peak reminds the reader that regulation and courage are developed over time. One calm moment is not proof that every underlying condition has been resolved.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible upright expressions include:

- patient and compassionate love;
- strong sexual or emotional energy handled with respect;
- de-escalation;
- helping one another feel safe without controlling;
- resilience through difficulty;
- honest anger managed without cruelty;
- or attraction grounded in trust.

In shadow it can indicate one person continually managing the other’s volatility, suppression of needs to maintain peace, controlling behavior framed as care, or chemistry that overwhelms boundaries.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- courageous;
- calm under pressure;
- compassionate;
- physically vital;
- patient;
- emotionally regulated;
- protective;
- confident without needing spectacle;
- or learning to integrate anger, sexuality, fear, or instinct.

Shadow possibilities include concealed rage, fragile self-control, pride, dominance, or dependence on another person to regulate them.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may contain:

- strong chemistry;
- healing through trust;
- an uneven but workable power difference;
- one person calming or encouraging another;
- shared resilience;
- or the need to create safer forms for intense emotion.

The card asks whether both people are free participants or whether one has become the other’s handler.

### Career, projects, and study

Strength favors:

- steady leadership;
- difficult conversations;
- crisis de-escalation;
- coaching;
- patient training;
- rehabilitation of a troubled project;
- and persistence without unnecessary aggression.

It warns against depending on one person’s emotional labor to contain a structurally unhealthy team or system.

### Money and material questions

The card can indicate disciplined spending, recovery from financial stress, patience with a long-term plan, resisting compulsive consumption, or negotiating firmly without escalation.

In shadow it may show denial, pride preventing assistance, or using money to control appetite or another person.

### Creativity

Strength supports making work from intense material without being consumed by it. It can mean sustained practice, trust in one’s voice, working with rather than against instinct, and tolerating vulnerability during revision.

Its shadow is overcontrolling the work until its vitality disappears.

### Spirituality and personal growth

The card can symbolize compassionate discipline, embodied courage, integration of shadow, and the recognition that instinct is not an enemy to destroy.

It warns against teachings that glorify suppression, obedience, sexual shame, or domination as spiritual mastery.

## Intentions

As intentions, Strength commonly indicates a desire to:

- remain patient;
- protect without overpowering;
- build trust;
- regulate a strong response;
- persevere;
- handle conflict gently;
- or express desire in a controlled and respectful way.

In shadow, the person may intend to manage, tame, or contain someone else rather than relate to them as an equal.

- confidence;
- patience;
- compassion;

- or intense emotion being held carefully.

## Actions / likely next move

Likely actions include:

- approaching calmly;
- de-escalating conflict;
- showing consistent support;
- setting a firm but compassionate boundary;
- continuing despite fatigue;
- or addressing desire or anger directly without acting destructively.

## Advice

Strength advises:

> **Meet the powerful feeling directly, neither shaming nor obeying it; use courage, proportion, touch, language, and boundaries to turn intensity into trustworthy action.**

Do not become solely responsible for containing another person’s danger.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card often indicates:

- regained confidence;
- successful de-escalation;
- resilience;
- healing relationship with instinct;
- steady progress;
- or a difficult force becoming cooperative without being destroyed.

It can also mean the outcome depends on patient maintenance rather than dramatic victory.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: Yes—especially for resilience, healing, courage, patient influence, and handling a difficult situation constructively.**

It is conditional where the situation requires structural protection rather than more personal endurance.

## Timing

Strength can indicate:

- gradual but steady development;
- after emotional or bodily regulation;
- during recovery or training;
- an eight-unit period when numerology is intentionally used;
- or a Leo-linked period when that overlay is deliberately applied.

The card favors sustainable pace over urgency.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, Strength may symbolize rehabilitation, nervous-system regulation, compassionate routine, rebuilding confidence, managing stress, or working safely with the body.

It should not be used to tell someone to endure abuse, ignore symptoms, skip treatment, or overcome illness through willpower. Sometimes strength means obtaining protection and qualified help.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Can this conflict be repaired?”

The hands at the jaws support direct, calm engagement before the conflict bites again. Repair is possible if anger can be named, boundaries are real, and neither person is assigned permanent responsibility for managing the other.

### Example: “Why am I drawn to this person?”

The lion may reflect vitality, sexuality, danger, confidence, or a disowned part of yourself. The floral bond suggests attraction can become relational, but only if intensity remains consensual and neither person is treated as an animal to tame.

### Example: “How do I finish this difficult work?”

The card favors patient repetition and embodied regulation rather than another burst of force. Work with the resistance, protect energy, and keep the goal in view like the distant mountain.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not equate strength with enduring abuse or carrying everyone else’s emotional regulation.
- Do not romanticize “taming” another person.
- Do not assume the woman is weak because her method is gentle or the lion is evil because it is instinctive.
- Do not claim the image clearly shows only opening or only closing the jaws; the gesture is visually ambiguous.
- Keep Leo/Teth correspondence identified as an overlay, not printed evidence.
- Verify flower placement, lion posture, colors, and background mountain against an actual 1909 Roses & Lilies scan.

## One-sentence essence

**True strength stays present to instinct without humiliation or surrender, turning raw intensity into courageous, bounded, and sustainable relationship.**

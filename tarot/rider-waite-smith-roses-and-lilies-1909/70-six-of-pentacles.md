# 70 — Six of Pentacles

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Minor Arcana
- **Suit:** Pentacles
- **Element:** Earth
- **Repository sequence number:** 70
- **Formal number:** 6
- **Printed inscription:** Roman numeral `VI` at the top; no printed card title appears on the face
- **Primary polarity:** distributing, measuring, unequal, materially relational
- **Later esoteric correspondence:** Moon in Taurus, traditionally titled Material Success; these attributions are not printed on the card
- **Core theme:** resources move between people through a visible power difference, requiring attention not only to generosity but also to measurement, eligibility, debt, dignity, reciprocity, control, and who defines what is enough
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded scan must be checked for edition, cropping, color, restoration, wear, numeral form, coin count, and small print differences before the literal description is treated as synchronized.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

A well-dressed standing figure occupies the center of the card. With one hand, the person distributes coins to a kneeling figure. With the other, the giver holds a small balance scale. A second kneeling figure waits nearby with open or raised hands.

Six golden pentacles appear across the upper field. The standing person wears a red outer garment over patterned or darker clothing, while the kneeling figures wear simpler garments in contrasting colors. Buildings or an urban environment appear in the distance.

The scene is not a circle of equal exchange. One person stands, measures, possesses, and decides; two people kneel and receive or wait. The card therefore depicts **material assistance inside a hierarchy**, where kindness and control can coexist.

### The standing giver

The central figure appears prosperous and socially established.

- The body is upright and stable.
- The clothing is richer and more layered than that of the kneeling figures.
- One hand reaches downward with coins.
- The other hand remains elevated with the scales.
- The figure occupies the visual center and controls both resource and measurement.

The giver may represent a benefactor, employer, lender, institution, government, family member, donor, client, manager, professional, or any person who controls access to money, time, opportunity, or care.

### The hand distributing coins

Coins fall or are placed into the hand of one kneeling figure.

- The gesture is concrete rather than symbolic.
- The recipient’s hand is open.
- The amount is limited and counted.
- The second recipient does not appear to receive at the same moment.

The giving hand can represent charity, wages, benefits, grant funding, repayment, patronage, support, tipping, allowance, or controlled distribution.

### The balance scales

A small pair of scales is held in the other hand.

- The beam appears approximately level.
- Two pans hang below it.
- The scale is portable and controlled by the giver.
- It introduces calculation into the act of giving.

The scales can symbolize fairness, budgeting, eligibility, debt, proportionality, law, means testing, exchange, or the criteria by which one person decides what another receives.

### The first kneeling figure

One person kneels closest to the giving hand.

- The hands are raised to receive.
- The posture is lower than the giver’s.
- The clothing appears modest.
- The figure participates actively by extending a hand.

This person may represent someone receiving aid, wages, repayment, approval, mentorship, access, or an opportunity. Kneeling should not be read as proof of inferiority, passivity, or moral debt.

### The second kneeling figure

Another person kneels beside or slightly behind the first.

- The hands may be open or joined.
- The figure waits while the other receives.
- The relationship to the giver remains unresolved.

This second figure makes comparison visible. It can signify different eligibility, unequal timing, multiple dependents, competition for scarce support, or the anxiety of not knowing whether one will also receive.

### The six pentacles

Six golden disks appear above the scene.

- They are displayed rather than physically held by the three people.
- Their arrangement creates a material canopy over the exchange.
- They can represent the total resource pool, visible wealth, six separate allocations, or the suit’s formal count.

The number six supports care, responsibility, relationship, and restoration, but the image asks whether the distribution process is genuinely equitable.

### The clothing and status contrast

The standing figure’s red garment and patterned layers contrast with the recipients’ simpler clothes.

- Red emphasizes agency, authority, and visibility.
- Rich textiles suggest status or surplus.
- The kneeling figures’ garments communicate need or reduced power.

The contrast can expose class, employer-worker relations, donor-recipient dynamics, family control, or institutional hierarchy.

### The distant buildings

An urban or built environment appears behind the figures.

- The exchange takes place within a social and economic system.
- The city suggests law, commerce, housing, work, and community.
- The figures are not isolated from wider structures.

This background reminds the reader that material inequality is not only personal. Systems determine who has assets, who applies, and who measures whom.

### The direction of gaze and attention

The giver’s attention appears directed toward the act of distribution and measurement. The kneeling figures focus upward or toward the offered coins.

- The visual flow runs downward from scale and hand to recipients.
- No reciprocal gift moves upward.
- The exchange may be temporary, contractual, charitable, or dependency-forming.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The principal relationships are:

- **gold:** money, aid, wages, opportunity, and measurable value;
- **red:** authority, action, visibility, and the power to decide;
- **blue, brown, and muted clothing:** need, humility, ordinary life, and material dependence;
- **gray or pale city:** the institutional and economic environment;
- **level scales:** the claim or aspiration of fairness.

The eye moves from the six pentacles to the scale, through the giver’s arms, and down toward the recipients. The hierarchy is built into the composition.

## Core meaning

The Six of Pentacles represents **resource exchange shaped by power, measurement, and responsibility**.

It can indicate generosity, aid, payment, fair distribution, philanthropy, benefits, patronage, debt, mentorship, lending, receiving support, or being in a position to help. The card can be favorable: material value is actually moving. But the scales and kneeling postures require the reader to ask who controls the terms, whether dignity is preserved, and whether assistance increases agency or dependency.

Waite’s historical list includes presents, gifts, gratification, attention, vigilance, the present moment, and prosperity. Reversed, he gives desire, cupidity, envy, jealousy, and illusion. Smith’s image supports this balanced synthesis:

> **Generosity is not measured only by what leaves the giver’s hand, but by fairness, consent, dignity, and what the exchange makes possible for the recipient.**

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `6` — care, responsibility, and relational repair

Six often concerns relationship, balance, and sustaining life.

It can indicate:

- redistribution;
- mutual responsibility;
- service;
- repayment;
- support after hardship;
- or the social systems that keep people materially connected.

Repeated Sixes can amplify caregiving, reciprocity, duty, harmony, or unequal responsibility.

### The scales — criteria and proportionality

The scale may symbolize:

- fairness;
- means testing;
- budgeting;
- legal entitlement;
- performance evaluation;
- or the giver’s private judgment.

A level scale does not prove that the criteria are just. Ask who designed the measure and whose needs it recognizes.

### The downward gift — material flow

The coins moving downward can represent:

- wages;
- benefits;
- charity;
- loan proceeds;
- repayment;
- allowance;
- sponsorship;
- or practical care.

The direction reveals hierarchy. It also shows that help must become usable, not remain a promise.

### The two recipients — comparison and unequal timing

The pair may indicate:

- two applicants;
- two dependents;
- different levels of need;
- unequal treatment;
- or the emotional effect of watching someone else receive.

Do not assume one is deserving and the other is not.

### The standing posture — control and capacity

Standing can signify:

- authority;
- surplus;
- decision rights;
- mobility;
- or responsibility for fair distribution.

Power increases accountability. It does not automatically make the giver benevolent.

### The urban background — systemic exchange

The buildings can represent:

- economy;
- government;
- company;
- family estate;
- institution;
- or community infrastructure.

They shift the reading from private generosity alone toward policy, access, and social distribution.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible upright expressions include:

- practical generosity;
- one partner supporting another temporarily;
- fair division of expenses and labor;
- gifts;
- caregiving;
- mentorship;
- or a relationship that benefits from explicit reciprocity.

The card may also reveal a power imbalance. One person may control money, housing, access, status, or professional authority. Generosity does not erase the need for consent and independent agency.

In shadow it can indicate love-bombing through gifts, financial control, unequal emotional labor, debt used as leverage, or giving in order to create obligation.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- generous;
- financially established;
- supportive;
- a donor, employer, lender, mentor, manager, or recipient;
- attentive to fairness;
- or accustomed to deciding how resources are distributed.

Shadow possibilities include paternalism, favoritism, control, underpayment, envy, or needing gratitude as proof of worth.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may involve:

- unequal resources;
- practical support;
- debt;
- mentorship;
- patronage;
- salary or client payment;
- caregiving;
- or a temporary asymmetry that needs explicit terms.

Ask whether support increases the lower-power person’s options and whether the arrangement can be renegotiated safely.

## Advice

The Six of Pentacles advises:

> **Make the exchange transparent enough that generosity does not become control and receiving does not require surrendering dignity.**

State the amount, criteria, duration, expectations, ownership, and exit conditions. Pay labor fairly. Give within capacity. Receive without turning temporary need into permanent shame.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card may indicate:

- aid received;
- payment made;
- debt addressed;
- fairer distribution;
- mentorship or sponsorship;
- or continued hierarchy within an otherwise helpful exchange.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: Yes for receiving, giving, funding, repayment, and practical support—provided the terms are fair and transparent.**

For questions about equal power, the answer may be “not yet.”

## Timing

The Six of Pentacles can indicate:

- when payment, approval, or eligibility is determined;
- at the present material exchange;
- six units of time when supported by the spread;
- during a grant, payroll, benefits, or repayment cycle;
- or after resources are measured and allocated.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, the Six may symbolize financial access to care, caregiving, practical assistance, disability support, benefits, equipment, transport, or fair division of support work.

It must not be used to imply that a patient, disabled person, dependent, or caregiver owes obedience or gratitude in exchange for necessary care.

## Spread interactions

The Six of Pentacles becomes especially significant with:

- **The Empress:** nourishment and care are materially expressed;
- **The Emperor:** centralized authority controls distribution;
- **The Hierophant:** institutional aid, scholarship, benefits, or formal mentorship;
- **Justice:** rights, criteria, contracts, and proportional fairness;
- **The Devil:** debt, coercion, or attachment may compromise the exchange;
- **the Five of Pentacles:** support follows hardship or remains inaccessible;
- **the Four of Pentacles:** concentrated assets meet redistribution;
- **the Seven of Pentacles:** giving becomes long-term investment;
- **the Ten of Pentacles:** family or institutional wealth is distributed;
- **the Six of Cups:** gifts, care, memory, and power differences overlap;
- **many Sixes:** care, responsibility, repayment, and relationship dominate;
- **many Pentacles:** resource distribution is the central issue.

Repeated scales, coins changing hands, kneeling and standing figures, gifts, open palms, cities, or groups of three can make fairness, hierarchy, gratitude, and material exchange spread-level themes.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Will I receive funding?”

The card supports distribution, but the scales show criteria and limited allocation. Verify eligibility, amount, timing, reporting duties, ownership, and whether the funder’s conditions preserve the project’s agency.

### Example: “Is this person genuinely generous?”

They may be materially helpful, but the image keeps them in control of both money and measure. Evaluate whether support is consistent, respectful, freely offered, and free from coercive expectations.

### Example: “How should we divide household costs?”

Use proportional rather than automatically identical contribution. Include income, unpaid labor, caregiving, disability, debt, and access to reserves. Make the method transparent and review it when circumstances change.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not assume the standing figure is benevolent merely because coins are given.
- Do not shame the kneeling figures or portray need as inferiority.
- Do not advise gifts, loans, or financial dependence without reviewing terms and safety.
- Do not treat generosity as consent to control another person.
- Do not turn Waite’s envy and jealousy vocabulary into unsupported accusations.
- Keep later Moon-in-Taurus correspondence identified as an overlay.
- Verify the Roman numeral, scale, six pentacles, coin distribution, clothing, postures, and city against the selected 1909 scan.

## One-sentence essence

**Let resources move, but measure the exchange by dignity, transparency, agency, and the real options it creates—not only by the giver’s intention.**

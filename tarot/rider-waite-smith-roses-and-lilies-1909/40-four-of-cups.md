# 40 — Four of Cups

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Minor Arcana
- **Suit:** Cups
- **Element:** Water
- **Repository sequence number:** 40
- **Formal number:** 4
- **Printed inscription:** Roman numeral `IV` at the top; no printed card title appears on the face
- **Primary polarity:** withdrawn, evaluating, emotionally paused, selectively receptive
- **Later esoteric correspondence:** Moon in Cancer, traditionally titled Luxury; these attributions are not printed on the card
- **Core theme:** emotional withdrawal creates space to distinguish genuine need from boredom, saturation, disappointment, or refusal to notice an available offer
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded scan must be checked for edition, cropping, color, restoration, wear, numeral form, and small print differences before the literal description is treated as synchronized.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

A solitary young figure sits cross-legged on a grassy rise beneath a tree. The arms are folded tightly across the chest, and the head tilts downward. Three golden cups stand upright on the ground in a row before the seated person.

From a gray-white cloud at the left, a pale hand extends a fourth golden cup toward the figure. The offered cup floats near shoulder or face height, clearly visible to the viewer but not visibly accepted.

The tree trunk rises behind the person and occupies the vertical center-right. Green leaves hang from the top border. The pale-blue sky and rolling green landscape remain open and quiet.

The composition is organized around **three cups already present, one cup newly offered, a closed body, and a tree that shelters while also enclosing the solitary mood**.

### The seated figure

The figure sits directly on the grass.

- The legs are crossed.
- The arms are folded across the chest.
- The shoulders curve inward.
- The head bends slightly downward.
- The gaze appears directed toward the ground or the three cups rather than the offered cup.
- The body remains still.

The posture can indicate contemplation, dissatisfaction, emotional self-protection, boredom, refusal, fatigue, meditation, or a deliberate pause before responding.

Folded arms are not proof of hostility. They can be a boundary, a need for internal processing, or a body protecting itself from further input.

### The clothing

The figure wears a muted green-gray tunic with red sleeves, blue trousers or leggings, and red-brown footwear.

- The green-gray torso blends with the tree and grass.
- Red sleeves place warmth or emotion inside the folded arms.
- Blue lower clothing reinforces the suit’s Water quality.
- The colors are less festive than those of the Three of Cups.

The clothing suggests emotional life remains present even though expression is closed. The person is not colorless; feeling has turned inward.

### The three cups on the ground

Three golden chalices stand upright before the figure.

- They are evenly spaced.
- They are within easy reach.
- None is spilled or broken.
- Their contents are not visible.
- The person does not touch them.

The cups may represent existing relationships, opportunities, experiences, comforts, achievements, or emotional options that have become familiar. Their upright condition matters: the card does not show total loss, only disengagement from what is already available.

### The offered fourth cup

A fourth golden cup is held out by a hand from a cloud.

- It is elevated above the three ground cups.
- It approaches from the left.
- Its rim remains open.
- It is presented without visible force.
- The figure does not reach for it.

The offered cup can represent a new invitation, emotional possibility, apology, insight, creative idea, friendship, or revised perspective. It may be valuable, unsuitable, mistimed, or simply not yet recognized.

The card does not require the figure to accept every offer. Discernment includes the right to decline.

### The hand and cloud

The hand emerges from a curling gray-white cloud in the same archetypal style used in the Aces.

- The palm supports the cup.
- The arm’s source is hidden.
- The gesture is calm rather than urgent.
- The cloud separates the offer from ordinary visible origins.

The offer may feel unexpected, intuitive, external, or not yet explained. It can also show an opportunity that the reader sees more clearly than the person inside the situation.

### The folded arms

The crossed arms form a horizontal barrier across the chest.

They can symbolize:

- emotional protection;
- refusal;
- self-containment;
- skepticism;
- saturation;
- or the need to feel one’s own response before receiving another person’s expectation.

In shadow, the barrier can become automatic rejection, stubbornness, or disengagement maintained after the original need for protection has passed.

### The tree trunk

A large tree rises behind the figure.

- The trunk is sturdy and rooted.
- It offers shade or shelter.
- The figure leans close to it without visibly touching it.
- Its vertical line contrasts with the closed horizontal arms.

The tree can represent stability, life experience, rootedness, isolation, or the protective structure under which reflection occurs. It may be a healthy retreat or a place where one becomes too comfortable to re-enter relationship.

### The hanging leaves

Green leaves fill the upper edge.

- They show that life continues above the withdrawn figure.
- They frame the Roman numeral.
- They create shade without darkening the whole scene.

The leaves suggest that emotional pause does not equal lifelessness. Growth may be happening internally or simply outside the figure’s current attention.

### The grassy rise and distant landscape

The person sits on a gently sloping green hill.

- Low trees or bushes appear in the distance.
- The horizon is open.
- No other people are present.
- The environment is safe enough for stillness.

The landscape gives the figure room to choose. The withdrawal is not visibly forced by immediate danger.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The principal relationships are:

- **gold:** emotional value and the four available vessels;
- **green:** rootedness, pause, and life continuing beneath withdrawal;
- **red:** contained feeling, irritation, warmth, or desire held inside the body;
- **blue:** reflection, sadness, emotional depth, and stillness;
- **gray-white cloud:** an offer whose origin or meaning is not fully known;
- **pale-blue sky:** openness outside the internal closure.

The viewer’s eye moves from the three cups to the folded figure, then left toward the offered cup and back to the closed arms. The composition creates a loop of **availability → withdrawal → offer → nonresponse**.

## Core meaning

The Four of Cups represents **emotional pause, selective attention, and the need to reassess what can still be received**.

It can describe boredom, dissatisfaction, withdrawal, contemplation, emotional saturation, missed opportunity, apathy, depression-like experience without diagnosis, or a necessary refusal to accept another offer before internal needs are understood.

Waite describes a young person under a tree contemplating three cups, while another is offered from a cloud. His meanings include weariness, disgust, aversion, imaginary vexations, and the possibility that the new cup is a fairy gift. Reversed, he gives novelty, presage, new instruction, and new relations.

The image does not say “take the cup” without question. It asks:

> **Are you declining because the offer is wrong, because the timing is wrong, or because withdrawal has narrowed what you can see?**

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `4` — container, stability, and emotional enclosure

Four creates structure.

It may indicate:

- emotional boundaries;
- home or routine;
- stability that has become stagnant;
- a protected pause;
- or the need to define what can enter one’s space.

Repeated Fours amplify structure, containment, security, and the risk of rigidity.

### Three existing cups — what is already available

The ground cups may symbolize:

- current relationships;
- prior offers;
- achievements;
- familiar pleasures;
- or emotional resources that no longer produce engagement.

Their upright position cautions against declaring that nothing remains.

### The fourth cup — difference, interruption, or another version

The offered cup may be:

- a new opportunity;
- an apology;
- a different emotional approach;
- creative inspiration;
- or the same pattern presented in a new form.

The reader should not assume novelty equals suitability.

### Crossed arms — boundary or closure

The arms can mean:

- “not now”;
- “not this”;
- “I need space”;
- self-protection;
- or emotional unavailability.

The distinction between boundary and avoidance depends on duration, context, and consequence.

### The downturned gaze — narrowed attention

The gaze may indicate:

- inward reflection;
- low mood;
- disappointment;
- fixation on what is missing;
- or concentration on an internal decision.

It can prevent the figure from noticing both the new offer and the living world around them.

### The tree — refuge and rooted habit

The tree can symbolize:

- safety;
- meditation;
- established identity;
- patience;
- or a retreat that has become habitual.

A tree grows slowly. The pause may be productive even when no outward movement is visible.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible upright expressions include:

- emotional withdrawal;
- one person needing space;
- a date or offer not accepted;
- dissatisfaction with the current pattern;
- taking a relationship for granted;
- not being ready for new love;
- or pausing to distinguish loneliness from genuine interest.

The card does not prove rejection. It may indicate timing, fatigue, grief, overstimulation, existing commitments, or a need for internal clarity.

In shadow it can show chronic disengagement, withholding as punishment, refusing every repair attempt, or expecting another person to keep offering without response.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- contemplative;
- dissatisfied;
- emotionally tired;
- guarded;
- selective;
- inwardly focused;
- unavailable at present;
- or reevaluating what they want.

Shadow possibilities include apathy, resentment, boredom-seeking novelty, passive refusal, or lack of awareness of support already present.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may involve:

- a pause;
- uneven engagement;
- one person making offers while the other withdraws;
- emotional routine losing vitality;
- or a need to renegotiate what closeness looks like.

The offered cup should not be used to pressure someone past a boundary. Mutual relationship requires the freedom to receive, decline, or ask for a different form.

### Career, projects, and study

The Four of Cups can indicate:

- disengagement;
- boredom with a role;
- overlooking an opportunity;
- creative or academic fatigue;
- dissatisfaction despite adequate conditions;
- or a need to stop accepting projects by default.

It favors reviewing values, workload, and the difference between temporary fatigue and structural misfit.

### Money and material questions

The card may show:

- indifference to an offer;
- missed financial opportunity;
- dissatisfaction that spending will not solve;
- adequate resources without emotional satisfaction;
- or declining terms that do not fit.

It cautions against impulse purchases used to escape boredom.

### Creativity

The card can describe:

- creative block;
- saturation with familiar ideas;
- a new concept not yet recognized;
- needed incubation;
- or refusal to produce before genuine interest returns.

The tree favors patience; the cloud-cup favors remaining open to an unexpected form.

### Spirituality and personal growth

The card may signify retreat, meditation, disenchantment, spiritual dryness, or questioning practices that once felt meaningful.

It can also warn against seeking constant revelation and overlooking quiet, ordinary forms of nourishment.

## Intentions

As intentions, the Four of Cups often shows a desire to:

- pause;
- withdraw;
- think;
- decline;
- avoid making an emotional commitment;
- reassess options;
- or wait for something that feels more authentic.

The intention may be self-protective rather than hostile.

## Feelings

As feelings, it can indicate:

- boredom;
- dissatisfaction;
- numbness;
- ambivalence;
- emotional fatigue;
- resentment;
- guarded curiosity;
- or a sense that available options do not reach the real need.

## Actions / likely next move

Likely actions include:

- delaying a response;
- declining an invitation;
- taking space;
- observing rather than participating;
- reviewing what has already been offered;
- or eventually noticing a new possibility.

Reversed, the person may re-engage, accept an offer, seek novelty, or abruptly leave a stagnant situation.

## Advice

The Four of Cups advises:

> **Honor the pause, but examine whether your boundary is protecting discernment or preserving disconnection after the danger has passed.**

Name what is missing. Review what remains. Look directly at the new offer without assuming you must accept it.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card may indicate:

- continued pause;
- an offer awaiting response;
- dissatisfaction prompting reevaluation;
- a missed opportunity;
- or a more conscious refusal that clears space for something better aligned.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: Not now, uncertain, or no to the current form.**

A conditional yes becomes possible if the person notices the offer and genuinely chooses it rather than responding from pressure or boredom.

## Timing

The Four of Cups can indicate:

- during a period of withdrawal or low engagement;
- after emotional saturation;
- four units of time when supported by the spread;
- when an overlooked offer becomes visible;
- or when rest has restored the capacity to choose.

Later Moon-in-Cancer symbolism emphasizes moods, memory, home, and fluctuating receptivity.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, the card may symbolize rest, emotional fatigue, reducing stimulation, seeking support, or noticing reduced pleasure and engagement.

It does not diagnose depression, burnout, trauma, or any condition. Persistent numbness, low mood, loss of pleasure, or inability to function deserves appropriate professional support.

## Spread interactions

The Four of Cups becomes especially significant with:

- **The Hermit:** chosen retreat, reflection, or deep isolation;
- **The High Priestess:** private processing and unspoken knowledge;
- **The Devil:** withdrawal may be tied to compulsion, resentment, or avoidance;
- **The Star:** replenishment and openness can return;
- **the Ace of Cups:** a genuine emotional opening is available;
- **the Two of Cups:** mutual connection may be offered but not yet received;
- **the Three of Cups:** social abundance contrasts with withdrawal;
- **the Five of Cups:** dissatisfaction may deepen into grief or loss;
- **the Eight of Cups:** pause becomes departure;
- **many Fours:** structure, boundaries, and stagnation dominate;
- **many Cups:** emotional saturation intensifies;
- **many Swords:** rumination or communication barriers shape the pause;
- **many Pentacles:** routine, work, or bodily conditions may underlie disengagement.

Repeated clouds, offered objects, crossed arms, trees, seated figures, or groups of three-plus-one can make refusal, opportunity, contemplation, and selective attention spread-level themes.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Why are they not responding?”

The folded arms and downturned gaze suggest withdrawal or internal processing. The offered cup shows contact may still be visible, but the card cannot identify motive. Give space, communicate once clearly, and do not turn silence into proof of hidden feeling.

### Example: “Should I accept this offer?”

The card advises neither automatic acceptance nor reflexive refusal. Compare the new cup with the three existing ones: is it genuinely different, or another version of the same dissatisfaction? Check practical terms and bodily response.

### Example: “Why can’t I enjoy what I achieved?”

The three upright cups show that the achievement exists, while the closed posture shows emotional contact with it is limited. Rest, grief, values mismatch, or saturation may need attention before another reward can feel meaningful.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not label someone ungrateful, lazy, depressed, or emotionally defective from this card alone.
- Do not pressure a person to accept an offer because it appears divinely presented.
- Do not treat silence as proof of secret love or permanent rejection.
- Do not assume the three existing cups are worthless; they remain upright.
- Do not confuse novelty with healing.
- Preserve Waite’s language of weariness and aversion without turning it into diagnosis.
- Keep later Moon-in-Cancer and Luxury correspondences identified as overlays, not printed facts.
- Verify numeral, cup placement, clothing colors, hand position, tree, landscape, and crop against the selected 1909 Roses & Lilies scan.

## One-sentence essence

**The heart pauses before another offer: respect the boundary, examine the dissatisfaction, and look clearly enough to know whether the cup is wrong, mistimed, or simply unseen.**

# 68 — Four of Pentacles

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Minor Arcana
- **Suit:** Pentacles
- **Element:** Earth
- **Repository sequence number:** 68
- **Formal number:** 4
- **Printed inscription:** Roman numeral `IV` at the top; no printed card title appears on the face
- **Primary polarity:** holding, protective, controlling, materially consolidated
- **Later esoteric correspondence:** Sun in Capricorn, traditionally titled Earthly Power or Power; these attributions are not printed on the card
- **Core theme:** material value has been gathered and secured, but the method of protection may preserve stability, restrict circulation, isolate the holder, or turn possession into a posture that controls the body and attention
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. ## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

A solitary crowned figure sits squarely in the foreground before a distant city. Four golden pentacles are placed in direct relation to the body: one rests on or above the crown, one is clasped tightly against the chest, and two lie beneath the feet.

The figure wears a dark outer garment over red clothing. The arms wrap around the central pentacle, while the feet press down on the lower two. The person’s posture is closed, frontal, and highly symmetrical. The city behind appears active and extensive, but the figure remains outside or apart from it.

The composition therefore presents **security achieved through concentrated possession, with circulation, movement, and social participation visibly constrained**.

### The seated figure

The figure appears adult, prosperous, and self-contained.

- The body faces the viewer directly.
- The shoulders round around the pentacle at the chest.
- The knees remain close to the body.
- The feet are positioned on separate disks.
- The expression is serious or guarded.
- The posture is stable but not relaxed.

The figure may represent an owner, saver, guardian, manager, institution, family member, landlord, employer, or any person whose security depends on controlling access to resources.

### The crown and upper pentacle

A pentacle sits on or just above the figure’s crown.

- It occupies the highest point of the body.
- It visually links wealth or possession with identity and thought.
- It may partly replace the open sky above the head.

This pentacle can symbolize status, self-definition through assets, preoccupation with security, a material responsibility carried mentally, or the belief that value must be visibly displayed to establish authority.

### The pentacle held at the chest

The central disk is clasped with both arms.

- It covers much of the torso.
- The hands and forearms protect it from both sides.
- It lies near the heart and breath.
- The grip prevents the arms from opening freely.

The chest pentacle can represent savings, property, emotional protection, a treasured role, a relationship treated as possession, or a resource whose loss feels personally threatening.

### The two pentacles beneath the feet

Each foot rests on a golden disk.

- The coins create a stable base.
- They also limit walking or turning.
- The figure appears to pin them in place.
- The body’s entire weight participates in holding them.

These pentacles can symbolize financial foundation, land, control of access, inherited assets, practical security, or resources that have become immobilized to prevent loss.

### The throne, stool, or stone seat

The figure sits on a simple block-like support.

- It is less ornate than the seats of the Major Arcana rulers.
- It keeps the person close to the ground.
- Its squared form reinforces stability and rigidity.

The seat can represent ownership, a fixed role, a defensive position, or a material structure that supports authority without necessarily creating belonging.

### The dark cloak and red clothing

The outer garment appears dark, while red or warm clothing shows beneath.

- The dark layer encloses the body.
- The red suggests vitality, desire, or fear of loss under the protective exterior.
- The contrast makes the central pentacle especially visible.

The clothing can indicate private anxiety beneath apparent control, or active will contained inside a conservative structure.

### The distant city

A dense city rises behind the figure.

- Towers, roofs, and buildings fill the horizon.
- The city is close enough to be relevant but separate from the immediate foreground.
- No other person appears beside the seated figure.
- The urban environment suggests exchange, community, work, commerce, and circulation.

The distance between figure and city can symbolize isolation, exclusion, private accumulation, responsibility at the edge of a system, or choosing security over participation.

### The closed bodily geometry

The body and coins form a compact, almost locked arrangement.

- Head, heart, and feet are all occupied by pentacles.
- Arms and legs have limited freedom.
- The posture creates a square or fortress-like silhouette.

The image asks what is being protected, what movement has been sacrificed, and whether the current boundary remains proportionate to the actual risk.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The principal relationships are:

- **gold:** property, security, visible value, status, and material responsibility;
- **black or dark cloth:** defense, privacy, fear, containment, and seriousness;
- **red:** desire, vitality, will, and attachment;
- **gray or pale city tones:** the wider social and economic system;
- **earth-toned foreground:** fixed position and bodily consequence.

Unlike the moving Two, the Four contains almost no visual circulation. The gaze repeatedly returns to the four coins and the body that holds them.

## Core meaning

The Four of Pentacles represents **material consolidation and the cost of holding tightly**.

It can indicate saving, protecting assets, maintaining boundaries, preserving privacy, securing a home, controlling spending, conserving energy, or stabilizing a vulnerable situation. These can be wise responses. The card becomes shadowed when protection turns into fear, possession, rigidity, exclusion, hoarding, or the belief that security requires control over other people.

Waite’s historical meanings include surety of possessions, cleaving to that which one has, gifts, legacy, and inheritance. Reversed, he gives suspense, delay, and opposition. Smith’s staging makes the bodily cost of the hold unmistakable:

> **What you protect can support you, but what you grip with your whole body can also prevent you from moving, receiving, and participating.**

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `4` — structure, containment, and fixed foundation

Four creates form and stability.

It can indicate:

- four walls;
- a budget;
- property;
- a fixed contract;
- a protected boundary;
- or a structure that resists movement.

Repeated Fours amplify stability, rules, home, control, delay, or rigidity.

### One pentacle at the head — material identity

The upper coin may symbolize:

- status consciousness;
- preoccupation with money;
- ownership defining self-worth;
- a responsibility that dominates thought;
- or a deliberate focus on security.

### One pentacle at the heart — emotional possession

The central coin may indicate:

- attachment to what feels safe;
- protecting a treasured resource;
- fear of vulnerability;
- emotional closure;
- or confusing care with ownership.

In relationship readings, this is a caution against treating a partner, child, client, or dependent as a possession.

### Two pentacles underfoot — secured foundation and immobilization

The lower coins can signify:

- property;
- reserves;
- stable income;
- land;
- or material footing.

They also show that a foundation can become a trap when the person cannot step away from it.

### The city — exchange beyond the private hold

The distant buildings may represent:

- community;
- market;
- family system;
- work;
- taxation;
- public life;
- or opportunities requiring participation and circulation.

### The closed posture — self-protection made visible

The bodily geometry can indicate:

- careful boundaries;
- vigilance;
- possessiveness;
- scarcity memory;
- or a nervous system organized around preventing loss.

It is descriptive, not diagnostic.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible upright expressions include:

- valuing stability;
- protecting a relationship’s privacy;
- setting financial boundaries;
- wanting exclusivity or security;
- moving slowly before opening;
- or holding on to a bond because it provides structure.

In shadow it can indicate possessiveness, jealousy, emotional withholding, controlling access, staying from fear rather than mutual growth, or treating commitment as ownership.

The card does not prove greed, abuse, or lack of love. Examine behavior, consent, and power in context.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- cautious;
- private;
- financially conservative;
- protective of property or time;
- stable;
- difficult to access;
- security-focused;
- or afraid of losing what they have built.

Shadow possibilities include controlling behavior, rigidity, territoriality, status attachment, emotional closure, or using resources to maintain power.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may involve:

- shared assets;
- boundaries;
- property;
- savings;
- fear of change;
- one person controlling access;
- or a stable arrangement with limited emotional movement.

Ask whether both people consent to the structure and whether security is shared or concentrated in one person’s hands.

### Career, projects, and study

The Four can indicate:

- protecting scope;
- conserving budget;
- retaining intellectual property;
- maintaining a stable role;
- guarding credentials or access;
- or avoiding unnecessary risk.

Its shadow appears as knowledge hoarding, micromanagement, refusal to invest, bottlenecks, territorial teams, or preserving a failing system because change feels dangerous.

### Money and material questions

The card strongly relates to:

- saving;
- reserves;
- property;
- legacy;
- inheritance;
- controlled spending;
- asset protection;
- and financial boundaries.

It favors security but asks whether money is serving life or organizing life around fear.

### Creativity

The card may describe:

- protecting unpublished work;
- retaining rights;
- limiting access;
- preserving materials;
- or stabilizing a creative practice.

In shadow it can show fear of sharing, perfectionism, or treating an idea as property that must never be changed through collaboration.

### Spirituality and personal growth

The Four can symbolize grounding, disciplined boundaries, and protection of private practice.

It can also reveal attachment to status, possessions, fixed identity, or certainty as a substitute for trust and participation.

## Advice

The Four of Pentacles advises:

> **Protect what matters, then check whether protection has become the only life your resources are allowed to have.**

Define a reserve, not an endless fortress. Separate ownership from identity. Build fair access, preserve consent, and allow value to circulate where circulation is safe and purposeful.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card may indicate:

- assets retained;
- a stable but closed arrangement;
- continued saving;
- possession or inheritance;
- limited movement;
- or a situation preserved at the cost of expansion.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: Yes for saving, securing, protecting, and maintaining; no or not yet for openness, movement, sharing, and expansion.**

## Timing

The Four of Pentacles can indicate:

- delay until security is established;
- a fixed or slow period;
- four units of time when supported by the spread;
- timing tied to property, payment, inheritance, or approval;
- or a situation that remains unchanged until someone deliberately loosens control.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, the Four may symbolize conserving energy, protecting recovery time, maintaining financial access to care, or creating stable routines and boundaries.

It should not be used to diagnose hoarding disorder, anxiety, rigidity, circulatory problems, or any other condition. If fear or financial control affects safety or care, practical and professional support matters more than divination.

## Spread interactions

The Four of Pentacles becomes especially significant with:

- **The Emperor:** authority, property, rules, and centralized control;
- **The Devil:** attachment, debt, coercive control, or fear-based possession;
- **Justice:** ownership, inheritance, contracts, and fair distribution;
- **Death:** the hold must change or end;
- **The Tower:** the protected structure may be disrupted;
- **the Ace of Pentacles:** a new resource is being secured;
- **the Two of Pentacles:** holding meets circulation and adaptation;
- **the Six of Pentacles:** possession meets distribution and power;
- **the Ten of Pentacles:** private assets enter a family or legacy system;
- **the Four of Cups:** emotional and material withholding reinforce one another;
- **many Fours:** stability, delay, home, boundaries, or rigidity dominate;
- **many Pentacles:** money, property, body, and resource control are central.

Repeated closed arms, crowns, seated figures, city distance, objects underfoot, gates, locks, or coins at the body can make possession, identity, access, and security spread-level themes.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Should I keep saving or invest?”

The card validates a reserve but warns against immobilizing every resource. Define emergency needs, time horizon, risk tolerance, fees, and diversification with appropriate financial guidance before deciding what portion can move.

### Example: “Why are they emotionally guarded?”

The chest-held pentacle suggests something valuable is being protected. That may be privacy, past security loss, current stress, or a deliberate boundary. The card does not grant access or prove hidden feelings; respect behavior and stated limits.

### Example: “What is blocking this team?”

Knowledge, budget, permissions, or decision power may be concentrated in one place. Identify bottlenecks, clarify ownership, and create controlled but workable circulation rather than demanding total openness.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not automatically label the figure greedy, miserly, abusive, or emotionally incapable.
- Do not diagnose hoarding disorder or anxiety from symbolic holding.
- Do not treat commitment or exclusivity as ownership of another person.
- Do not advise sharing resources where safety, consent, fraud risk, or coercive control is present.
- Preserve Waite’s legacy and inheritance meanings without predicting a death or inheritance event.
- Keep later Sun-in-Capricorn correspondence identified as an overlay.
- Verify the Roman numeral, city, crown coin, chest coin, foot coins, clothing, and seat against the selected 1909 scan.

## One-sentence essence

**Secure the foundation without letting fear turn value into a fortress that controls your body, identity, and access to the wider world.**

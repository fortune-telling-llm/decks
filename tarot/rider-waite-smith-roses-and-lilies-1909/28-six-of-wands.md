# 28 — Six of Wands

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Minor Arcana
- **Suit:** Wands
- **Element:** Fire
- **Repository sequence number:** 28
- **Formal number:** 6
- **Printed title:** `SIX of WANDS.`
- **Primary polarity:** visible, victorious, acknowledged, socially elevated
- **Later esoteric correspondence:** Jupiter in Leo, traditionally titled Victory; these attributions are not printed on the card
- **Core theme:** effort or identity receives public recognition, creating momentum and influence while testing whether acclaim is supported by substance, loyalty, and continued responsibility
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded scan must be checked for edition, cropping, color, restoration, wear, and small print differences before the literal description is treated as synchronized.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

A mounted figure occupies the center of the card and moves through a group of attendants or followers. The rider wears a laurel wreath and holds one upright wand crowned with another wreath. Five additional wands are carried by the surrounding people, completing the formal count of six.

The horse is pale and partly covered by a green cloth. The rider’s warm-colored clothing and red-orange mantle stand out above the crowd. The surrounding figures are smaller and less individually detailed, making the rider the clear center of attention.

The composition is processional rather than private. The eye moves through **wreath on head → wreath on wand → rider → horse → surrounding staffs and people**. Recognition is doubled: the person is crowned, and the emblem of will is crowned too.

### The rider

The central person sits upright on the horse.

- A green laurel wreath encircles the head.
- The posture is composed and elevated.
- The face is shown in profile or three-quarter view rather than looking directly at the viewer.
- Warm red, orange, or yellow clothing marks the figure as visually prominent.
- The rider controls or accompanies the horse without visible struggle.

Elevation gives the rider status, visibility, and a different perspective from the people on foot. It can signify earned leadership, public office, temporary celebrity, or the social distance that success may create.

### The crowned wand

The rider holds one tall living staff upright.

- A laurel wreath is attached near its top.
- Green leaves remain visible on the wood.
- The staff rises above the rider and crowd.
- The wreath transforms a personal tool into a public standard or victory emblem.

The wand can represent the project, message, campaign, identity, or cause being celebrated. Recognition is not only personal; it attaches to what the rider carried forward.

### The laurel wreath on the head

The second wreath marks the rider directly.

- It is green and living rather than metallic.
- It visually links the person to the leafy wands.
- Its circular form contrasts with the staffs’ vertical lines.

The wreath can symbolize victory, reputation, approval, completion of a contest, or the right to be heard. Because it is bestowed or socially legible, it also makes success dependent on an audience.

### The horse

The rider sits on a pale horse partly draped in green cloth.

- The horse’s head and body are stylized and somewhat unusual in proportion.
- It moves with less violent force than the Knight of Wands’ rearing horse.
- The green covering links motion with growth, public ceremony, or controlled vitality.
- The animal carries the rider through the group.

The horse represents embodied momentum and the practical force beneath public status. The acclaim may belong to the rider, but movement depends on support, training, labor, and conditions that are less visible.

### The surrounding figures

Several people walk around or behind the rider.

- Their faces and clothing are less prominent.
- Each visible staff contributes to the full count.
- Some appear to look toward the rider or procession.
- They may be supporters, foot soldiers, colleagues, witnesses, messengers, or members of the public.

The group can signify community recognition, team support, constituency, audience, or the people whose labor helped produce the victory. It can also raise questions about hierarchy, loyalty, and whether the acclaim is genuinely shared.

### The six wands

The staffs form a vertical rhythm across the card.

- One is crowned and held by the rider.
- Five are carried by the surrounding figures.
- All remain living and leafy.
- Their parallel direction creates more coordination than the crossed staffs of the Five.

The transition from Five to Six is visible: competing energies have aligned around one result, message, or leader.

### The processional space

The card provides little distant landscape.

- The figures fill the scene.
- Attention is social rather than geographic.
- Movement appears to occur through a public route or gathering.
- The outcome is visible to others.

This can correspond to awards, announcements, promotions, elections, successful presentations, public praise, or any situation where recognition changes social position.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The principal relationships are:

- **green:** living achievement, growth, the laurel, and support beneath visibility;
- **red and orange:** confidence, status, Fire, and public attention;
- **pale horse and sky:** a field against which the rider becomes legible;
- **brown staffs:** individual effort aligned into a common direction.

The repeated vertical staffs lift the image, while the procession moves laterally. The card combines upward status with forward momentum.

## Core meaning

The Six of Wands represents **recognition after contest or effort**.

It can indicate victory, promotion, praise, good news, restored confidence, a successful campaign, public acknowledgment, influence, leadership, or an expectation fulfilled. It is especially relevant when a result matters because other people see, validate, or follow it.

Waite describes a laurelled horseman bearing a staff with a laurel crown and attendants at his side. He includes both the victor in triumph and the state courier carrying great news. His meanings include expectation crowned by its own desire and the crown of hope. Reversed, he lists apprehension, fear, treachery, disloyalty, and indefinite delay.

The image-led message is:

> **Accept recognition, remember the people and structures carrying it, and use visibility as responsibility rather than proof of permanent superiority.**

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `6` — alignment, relationship, and restored order

Six often reorganizes the conflict of Five into cooperation, responsibility, and a workable relationship among parts.

It may indicate:

- a group aligning around one result;
- recognition from others;
- service and leadership;
- harmony after competition;
- or the responsibilities created by success.

Repeated Sixes amplify reciprocity, care, social consequence, support, and the need to sustain what has been achieved.

### The elevated rider — status and perspective

The rider’s height can signify:

- leadership;
- visibility;
- promotion;
- authority;
- representation of a group;
- or temporary social elevation.

In shadow it can become superiority, distance from the team, or confusion between being seen and being right.

### Two laurel wreaths — person and achievement

One wreath crowns the person; the other crowns the wand.

This can distinguish:

- recognition of character versus recognition of a result;
- personal reputation versus project success;
- the leader versus the cause;
- or a moment when the two have become publicly linked.

If the project later fails, the person’s identity may be affected because the symbols are joined.

### The horse — momentum supported by unseen labor

The horse may represent:

- a vehicle, platform, institution, team, body, or system carrying the visible figure;
- confidence in motion;
- trained energy;
- or the practical foundation beneath acclaim.

The rider should not claim the horse’s labor as personal brilliance.

### The attendants — support, audience, and hierarchy

The surrounding people can indicate:

- colleagues;
- followers;
- voters;
- family;
- friends;
- customers;
- audience;
- or people whose confidence makes leadership possible.

The card asks whether they are recognized, consulted, and treated fairly.

### The aligned staffs — coordination after conflict

The staffs no longer collide as in the Five.

They may symbolize:

- team alignment;
- a shared message;
- common direction;
- organized effort;
- or many wills temporarily following one standard.

Alignment can be sincere or merely conformist. Surrounding cards reveal whether dissent has been integrated or silenced.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible upright expressions include:

- feeling chosen or proudly acknowledged;
- a relationship becoming public;
- one partner celebrating the other;
- successful pursuit;
- confidence restored after relational difficulty;
- supportive friends or family;
- or a couple reaching a visible milestone.

The card can also describe courtship as conquest, attention-seeking, or competition for approval. It does not prove exclusivity, emotional depth, or durable intimacy.

In shadow it may indicate one partner needing constant praise, public image outweighing private care, jealousy of another’s success, or feeling unseen while the other receives acclaim.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- confident;
- successful;
- publicly visible;
- persuasive;
- proud;
- receiving praise;
- carrying news;
- or seeking acknowledgment and influence.

Shadow possibilities include vanity, insecurity beneath performance, arrogance, reputation management, or claiming collective work as personal victory.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may involve:

- mutual encouragement;
- one person championing the other;
- public acknowledgment;
- shared success;
- or a dynamic shaped by status and admiration.

The rider-and-crowd composition can also reveal unequal visibility. One person may be celebrated while the other provides support from the ground.

### Career, projects, and study

The Six of Wands is favorable for:

- promotion;
- passing an assessment;
- receiving an award;
- successful launch or presentation;
- positive reviews;
- election or appointment;
- stakeholder approval;
- public communication;
- and morale after a difficult competition.

Its caution is sustainability. Recognition may create new expectations, leadership duties, and scrutiny.

### Money and material questions

The card may indicate:

- financial improvement linked to success or visibility;
- a profitable launch;
- recognition increasing opportunities;
- or good news about an application, bid, or sale.

It does not guarantee long-term wealth. Public success can also increase spending, obligations, or pressure to maintain appearances.

### Creativity

The card can describe:

- applause;
- publication;
- favorable reception;
- an award;
- performance success;
- audience growth;
- or confidence to continue after being seen.

### Spirituality and personal growth

The card may symbolize restored confidence, visible service, community trust, or a teaching role.

Its shadow is spiritual status, charisma mistaken for wisdom, or dependency on being admired as advanced, chosen, or authoritative.

## Intentions

As intentions, the Six of Wands often shows a desire to:

- succeed visibly;
- receive approval;
- announce good news;
- claim a role;
- lead;
- be chosen;
- make a relationship public;
- or return after proving something.

The intention may include genuine contribution and a strong need for acknowledgment at the same time.

## Feelings

As feelings, it can indicate:

- pride;
- admiration;
- confidence;
- relief;
- feeling recognized;
- desire to impress;
- hope that attention is reciprocated;
- or fear of losing status once gained.

## Actions / likely next move

Likely actions include:

- announcing a result;
- approaching with confidence;
- accepting recognition;
- leading a group;
- presenting publicly;
- asking for support;
- or making a success visible.

Reversed, the person may delay, retreat after criticism, seek reassurance, exaggerate achievements, or discover that support is less secure than expected.

## Advice

The Six of Wands advises:

> **Receive the victory without forgetting the horse, the attendants, the contest, and the work that begins after applause.**

Share credit, verify the result, and use increased visibility to support rather than dominate others.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card commonly indicates:

- recognition;
- favorable news;
- promotion;
- victory in a contest;
- increased influence;
- restored confidence;
- or a public result that advances the situation.

The victory may be a stage rather than the final destination.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: Yes—especially for recognition, promotion, public support, successful presentation, and favorable news.**

For questions about lasting loyalty or private emotional depth, additional cards are needed.

## Timing

The Six of Wands can indicate:

- when results are announced;
- after a contest, review, campaign, or presentation;
- six units of time when supported by the spread;
- during a highly visible period;
- or once a group’s support becomes public.

Later Jupiter-in-Leo symbolism favors expansion and recognition, but timing remains contextual.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, the card may symbolize encouraging progress, restored confidence, successful completion of a supportive goal, or community acknowledgment of effort.

Its shadow cautions against performing wellness for approval, overexerting to preserve an image, dismissing ongoing needs after one improvement, or treating praise as medical evidence.

## Spread interactions

The Six of Wands becomes especially significant with:

- **The Magician:** skill receives visible proof or recognition;
- **The Emperor:** promotion, authority, command, or institutional status;
- **The Chariot:** disciplined victory and public forward movement;
- **Strength:** confidence grounded in self-regulation rather than applause;
- **Justice:** official result, award, judgment, or accountability;
- **The Sun:** highly visible success and morale;
- **the Five of Wands:** competition produces a winner or public result;
- **the Seven of Wands:** victory must be defended;
- **the Ten of Wands:** recognition creates burden and responsibility;
- **many Sixes:** support, service, and social reciprocity dominate;
- **many Cups:** emotional approval and belonging matter;
- **many Pentacles:** recognition has practical or financial consequence;
- **many Swords:** public narrative, criticism, or contested judgment shapes the victory.

Repeated wreaths, horses, elevated figures, crowds, standards, messengers, or aligned staffs can make recognition, leadership, and social support spread-level themes.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Will I get the promotion?”

The elevated rider and two wreaths strongly support recognition or favorable news. The surrounding people indicate that trust and group perception matter. The card is positive, while later cards should assess workload, terms, and whether the role is sustainable.

### Example: “How does this person feel about me?”

They may admire you, feel proud to be associated with you, or want your approval. The image does not prove private love. It can also show that the person enjoys being seen as successful in the connection.

### Example: “Why does success feel uncomfortable?”

The card places one person above a crowd. Visibility can create scrutiny, impostor feelings, debt to supporters, or fear of falling. Integrate the recognition without making it the sole measure of worth.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not equate acclaim with moral correctness or lasting success.
- Do not erase the attendants, team, audience, or horse beneath the visible figure.
- Do not infer treachery from a reversal without evidence.
- Do not treat public acknowledgment as proof of private commitment.
- Do not encourage superiority, humiliation of competitors, or dependence on applause.
- Keep Jupiter-in-Leo and Victory identified as later overlays rather than printed evidence.
- Verify the horse, garment colors, wreaths, staff count, figures, and title against the exact 1909 scan selected for the repository.

## One-sentence essence

**Recognition has lifted the work into public view: accept the victory, share its credit, and let visibility deepen responsibility rather than inflate identity.**

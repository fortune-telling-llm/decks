# 22 — XXI — The World

## Card metadata

- **Deck:** Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot — Original Roses & Lilies Edition (1909)
- **Arcana:** Major Arcana
- **Repository sequence number:** 22
- **Historical card number:** XXI
- **Printed title:** `THE WORLD.`
- **Primary polarity:** completing, integrating, participating, encompassing
- **Primary esoteric correspondence:** Saturn and the Hebrew letter Tau in the Golden Dawn/Waite–Smith system; neither attribution is printed on the card
- **Core theme:** a cycle becomes whole when its many parts can be embodied, related, and carried forward within a completed form that remains open to the next movement
- **Artwork status:** this description targets Pamela Colman Smith’s first 1909 Roses & Lilies design. Any uploaded image must be checked against an early-edition scan for exact wreath leaves, ribbons, batons, sash, figure pose, living creatures, cloud forms, color, linework, cropping, restoration, and print wear.

## Literal visual description

### Overall composition

The card is vertically oriented with the Roman numeral **`XXI`** at the top and **`THE WORLD.`** in the lower title panel. A nude or partly veiled human figure dances or floats at the center of a large oval green wreath. A purple sash spirals around the body. The figure holds a short baton or wand in each hand.

Red ribbons bind or decorate the wreath at its upper and lower points. In the four corners, winged or cloud-supported beings appear: a human or angelic face, an eagle, a lion, and a bull. The background is blue and open.

The composition is centered, circular, and balanced. The wreath encloses the dancer without functioning as a prison. The four corner beings stabilize the outer field while the central body remains in motion.

### The central dancing figure

The figure occupies the exact center of the wreath.

- The body is nude except for a flowing purple sash.
- One leg bears the weight or extends downward while the other crosses behind or in front, creating a pose that echoes The Hanged Man in a liberated orientation.
- The arms extend outward and slightly upward.
- The head turns toward one side rather than facing rigidly forward.
- The posture suggests dance, rotation, balance, and active participation.
- The figure’s gender presentation should not be overfixed; historical commentators have described the body in different gendered or androgynous terms.

The central body is not enthroned. Completion is represented through movement and embodied participation rather than static rule.

### The two batons

The figure holds one short baton in each hand.

- The objects are similar in size.
- Each is held openly rather than used as a weapon.
- Their paired placement balances the body’s two sides.
- They resemble tools of direction, performance, ritual, or completed agency.

The two batons can symbolize mastery of more than one mode, balanced agency, the ability to act in relationship, or the integration of beginning and completion. They may also echo the Magician’s single raised wand, now doubled and distributed across both hands.

### The purple sash

A long purple cloth winds around the central figure.

- It covers part of the body without hiding its form.
- It moves in a curved line that reinforces dance and rotation.
- Its color contrasts with the green wreath and blue background.
- It creates a visible path around the body rather than a fixed garment.

The sash can symbolize continuity, dignity, spirit moving through matter, memory carried through completion, or the living thread that prevents wholeness from becoming rigid perfection.

### The crossed legs

The figure’s legs form an asymmetrical crossed shape.

- The pose recalls the inverted geometry of The Hanged Man.
- Here the figure is upright or freely suspended rather than tied.
- The crossing creates movement within the centered oval.

This echo can signify that the perspective and surrender of The Hanged Man have been integrated into free action. What was once suspension becomes dance.

### The oval wreath

A thick green wreath surrounds the central figure.

- It is made from living leaves.
- Its shape is elongated rather than perfectly circular.
- It creates a complete boundary with visible inner space.
- The dancer does not touch every part of it.
- Red ribbons secure or mark its top and bottom.

The wreath can symbolize completion, achievement, protection, the world-egg, a portal, the boundary of one finished cycle, or a living system capable of holding complexity.

Because it is organic and tied rather than carved in stone, the completed form remains flexible and alive.

### The red ribbons

Red ribbons appear at the upper and lower points of the wreath.

- They gather or secure the greenery.
- Their shapes may resemble infinity loops or crossed bindings depending on the scan.
- They create two transition points within the oval.

The ribbons can symbolize connection, sealing, vitality, covenant, repeated cycle, or the points at which completion opens into another beginning.

### The human or angelic being

A winged human or angelic face appears in one upper corner among clouds.

- The face looks inward toward the central field.
- Wings or cloud forms frame it.
- It provides a conscious, relational, or communicative witness.

This being can symbolize Air, Aquarius, humanity, thought, communication, or a fixed perspective that observes the whole.

### The eagle

An eagle appears in the opposite upper corner.

- Its head is shown in profile or three-quarter view.
- It emerges from cloud.
- It looks toward the center.

The eagle can symbolize vision, height, transformation, Scorpio in fixed-sign correspondence, penetrating perception, or the capacity to see a wider pattern.

### The lion

A lion occupies one lower corner.

- Its mane frames the head.
- It emerges from cloud.
- Its gaze is directed inward.

The lion can symbolize vitality, courage, Fire, Leo, creative force, or embodied will integrated into the larger order.

### The bull

A bull appears in the remaining lower corner.

- Horns and broad head identify it.
- It emerges from cloud.
- It faces toward the central scene.

The bull can symbolize Earth, Taurus, stability, body, labor, material continuity, and the capacity to sustain the completed form.

### The four living beings as a whole

The four corner beings echo those surrounding Wheel of Fortune.

- In Wheel of Fortune they hold open books around a changing center.
- In The World they appear as stable witnesses around a completed, dancing center.
- They create fourfold balance without immobilizing the figure.

They can represent the four elements, fixed signs, directions, evangelists, living creatures of visionary texts, or the stable domains required for full participation. Specific correspondences should be identified as interpretive systems, not as printed labels.

### The blue background and clouds

The background is open blue with pale clouds around the corner beings.

- No ordinary ground is visible.
- The wreath and dancer appear suspended in a larger field.
- The open space prevents the completion from feeling crowded.

Blue can signify depth, distance, world, sky, spirit, and the spacious context in which the finished cycle is held.

### Color relationships and visual movement

The dominant relationships are:

- **green wreath:** living completion, boundary, growth, and continuity;
- **purple sash:** dignity, integration, movement, and a thread of meaning through embodiment;
- **red ribbons:** vitality, connection, sealing, and renewed cycle;
- **blue field:** spaciousness, world, distance, and encompassing context;
- **four creature colors and forms:** differentiated domains held around one center.

The eye circles the wreath, visits each corner being, returns to the dancer’s crossed legs and open arms, and follows the sash through the center. Completion is experienced as circulation rather than a final stop.

## Core meaning

The World represents **completion, integration, embodied participation, successful passage, and a cycle becoming whole enough to be carried forward**.

It can indicate achievement, graduation, travel, migration, public completion, mastery, belonging, synthesis, fulfillment, international or networked connection, a project delivered, or the recognition that formerly separate parts now form one coherent life or system.

The card is not a promise of permanent perfection. The dancer still moves, the wreath is living, and the red ribbons mark transition points. Completion creates a new level of responsibility and often opens the next cycle.

Its healthy expression is wholeness without erasure: body, mind, emotion, material reality, instinct, community, and purpose can participate without one claiming to be the entire world. Its shadow includes stagnation after success, inability to finish, perfectionism, feeling excluded from belonging, global ambition without local grounding, or using “completion” to suppress unfinished grief and maintenance.

Waite’s published divinatory range includes assured success, recompense, voyage, route, emigration, flight, and change of place; his reversed range includes inertia, fixity, stagnation, and permanence. These meanings fit the image’s central contrast between living completion and a completed form that becomes too fixed to move.

## Symbol-by-symbol interpretation

### `XXI` — complete sequence and return to openness

The number `21` completes the numbered Major Arcana. Reduced to `3`, it also carries creation, synthesis, and expression.

It can indicate:

- culmination;
- the third form created by integrating opposites;
- public or social completion;
- or readiness to return to The Fool’s openness at a more experienced level.

Repeated twenty-ones, threes, or completion cards can amplify graduation, collaboration, travel, and the need to finish before beginning again.

### The dancer — wholeness as movement

The central figure can represent:

- embodied self;
- a completed project;
- the querent participating fully;
- a system functioning as a whole;
- or identity no longer fragmented by incompatible roles.

Dance suggests responsiveness. Mastery is not frozen control but the ability to move within the whole.

### The two batons — balanced agency

The paired tools may indicate:

- competence across two domains;
- reciprocal action;
- beginning and ending held together;
- or authority distributed rather than concentrated in one hand.

### The sash — continuity through change

The cloth can symbolize the story, memory, relationship, or principle that passes through every stage while changing form.

It preserves continuity without becoming a chain.

### The wreath — completed living boundary

The wreath may represent:

- a finished body of work;
- graduation;
- healthy containment;
- a world or ecosystem;
- a portal;
- or the circle of participation within which the person now belongs.

Its green leaves show that maintenance and future growth remain necessary.

### The red ribbons — closure and reopening

The ribbons can indicate the points at which a cycle is tied off and handed into another. They may also echo infinity, suggesting completion within recurrence rather than absolute finality.

### The four beings — stable plurality

The corner figures show that wholeness includes different modes:

- thought and relationship;
- vision and transformation;
- vitality and creativity;
- body and material endurance.

No single creature occupies the center. The whole depends on differentiated support.

### The open sky — a larger context remains

Even a completed wreath floats inside a wider field. This can symbolize humility: every finished system remains part of something larger.

## Divinatory interpretation by question type

### Love and relationships

Possible upright expressions include:

- a relationship reaching mature integration;
- commitment or completion of a major stage;
- shared travel or relocation;
- a bond becoming part of a wider life;
- healthy closure;
- long-distance or international connection;
- or two people feeling fully able to participate as themselves.

The card can indicate union, but it does not guarantee marriage, permanence, or the absence of future work. In shadow it can show a relationship nearly complete, incompatible worlds, distance, or staying inside an identity that has finished its purpose.

### When asking about a specific person

The person may be:

- accomplished;
- integrated;
- worldly;
- widely connected;
- traveling or relocating;
- completing education or a major project;
- comfortable across cultures or domains;
- or closing one life cycle before beginning another.

Shadow possibilities include perfectionism, emotional distance through constant global movement, inability to settle, or identity overly dependent on achievement.

### The relationship between two people

The bond may contain:

- completion;
- mutual integration;
- shared belonging;
- a full cycle lived together;
- international or networked context;
- or a respectful ending that preserves meaning.

The wreath asks whether both people are included in the completed form or whether one has been placed outside the boundary.

### Career, projects, and study

The World strongly favors:

- project completion;
- release;
- graduation;
- certification;
- deployment;
- international work;
- successful integration;
- platform or ecosystem thinking;
- migration completed;
- and documenting a system so it can outlast the builders.

It warns against declaring success before maintenance, accessibility, handover, and excluded stakeholders are addressed.

### Money and material questions

The card can indicate completion of a financial goal, international transaction, property or relocation milestone, diversified system, retirement, or a mature asset reaching its intended use.

It is not a guarantee of unlimited wealth. Success still requires maintenance, taxes, legal compliance, and realistic transition planning.

### Creativity

The World is excellent for finishing, publishing, exhibiting, touring, integrating a body of work, or recognizing a coherent artistic identity.

Its shadow is perfectionism, endless polishing, fear of release, or treating one completed work as the only identity the artist can ever inhabit.

### Spirituality and personal growth

The card can symbolize integration, embodiment, participation in a larger whole, pilgrimage completed, and the recognition that spiritual insight must include ordinary life and diverse forms of being.

It warns against claims of total enlightenment, superiority, or universal authority. Wholeness is not omniscience.

## Intentions

As intentions, The World commonly indicates a desire to:

- complete;
- integrate;
- travel;
- formalize the finished result;
- bring all parts together;
- close a cycle well;
- share work publicly;
- or participate at a larger scale.

In shadow, the intention may be to preserve a completed identity and avoid the vulnerability of beginning again.

As feelings, it can show:

- fulfillment;
- belonging;
- pride;
- gratitude;
- mature love;
- satisfaction;
- spaciousness;
- or the sense that a relationship or chapter forms a complete world of meaning.

It can also include bittersweet emotion because completion requires release.

## Actions / likely next move

Likely actions include:

- finishing;
- publishing;
- graduating;
- traveling or relocating;
- delivering a final version;
- celebrating;
- integrating separate systems;
- closing a chapter;
- or preparing for the next cycle.

In shadow, action may stall at the last step or preserve the finished form beyond its useful life.

## Advice

The World advises:

> **Finish the cycle completely enough that every essential part can participate, celebrate what has been integrated, document what must endure, and leave the living boundary flexible enough for the next beginning.**

Completion is a responsibility, not merely a feeling.

## Outcome

As an outcome, the card often indicates:

- successful completion;
- achievement;
- integration;
- travel or relocation;
- public release;
- graduation;
- belonging;
- or a cycle closing with its meaning intact.

It is one of the deck’s strongest completion cards, while still implying the next movement beyond the wreath.

## Yes / no

**Leaning: Strong yes for completion, success, integration, travel, release, and fulfillment.**

It is conditional where the question assumes that completion means permanent stasis, universal approval, or freedom from maintenance.

## Timing

The World can indicate:

- at the end of the full cycle;
- after the final integration or handover;
- at graduation, release, arrival, or return;
- a twenty-one-unit period when numerology is intentionally used;
- or a Saturn-linked period when that overlay is deliberately applied.

It often means “when the last essential part is included,” not merely when most work is done.

## Health / wellbeing readings

Tarot is not a medical diagnosis. In a non-diagnostic wellbeing context, The World may symbolize completion of rehabilitation, coordinated whole-person care, adapting to a new baseline, travel considerations, or integrating physical, emotional, social, and practical support.

It must not be used to promise cure or a perfect body. Completion may mean living fully within changed capacity rather than returning to a former state.

## Image-led reading examples

### Example: “Is this project truly finished?”

The wreath supports completion only if the four corners are present: users, operations, documentation, and material support—or the equivalent domains in the project. The dancer can move because the whole system holds. A feature-complete product without maintenance or handover is not yet The World.

### Example: “What is the potential of this relationship?”

The card can show mature integration, shared travel, or a bond that becomes part of a wider life. It can also mark a complete and meaningful cycle whose healthiest form is closure. Surrounding cards and mutual choices determine which.

### Example: “Why am I afraid to release my work?”

The red ribbons show that completion requires tying off one form. The Fool waits beyond the sequence. Publishing does not trap you in this identity; it creates the space to begin again with what you have learned.

## Historical and fortune-teller cautions

- Do not promise permanent perfection, universal success, marriage, or total enlightenment solely from this card.
- Do not overassign the central figure’s gender; describe the visible body and acknowledge historical variation in interpretation.
- Do not treat global reach as inherently ethical or complete; ask who remains outside the wreath.
- Do not confuse closure with denial of unfinished grief or maintenance.
- Preserve the oval green wreath, red ribbons, purple sash, two batons, crossed-leg dance, four corner beings, clouds, and open blue field as visible details.
- Keep Saturn/Tau correspondence identified as an overlay, not printed evidence.
- Verify figure pose, sash path, creature arrangement, and wreath details against an actual 1909 Roses & Lilies scan.

## One-sentence essence

**A cycle becomes whole when its many parts can move together inside a living boundary, complete enough to celebrate and flexible enough to open into the next beginning.**

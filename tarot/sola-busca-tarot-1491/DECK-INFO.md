# Sola Busca Tarot (1491)

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** Sola Busca Tarot (1491)  
**Deck family:** Tarot  
**Historical edition:** Complete late-fifteenth-century Italian engraved tarot, conventionally dated 1491  
**Status:** Foundation documented; no card-specific Markdown or artwork files added in this task  
**Historical structure:** 78 cards: Mato plus 21 named trumps and 56 fully illustrated suit cards in Wands, Cups, Swords, and Coins  
**Primary reading style:** historical and image-led reading of a complete Renaissance narrative pack, with classical and biblical identification separated from later tarot correspondences

## Core concept

The Sola Busca is the earliest completely surviving 78-card tarot known and the earliest surviving full pack in which every suit card carries a figurative scene. Its trumps replace familiar archetypal titles with named ancient, legendary, and biblical figures, creating a historical-military-alchemical visual programme.

This repository entry documents a particular historical deck or edition, not a generic modern tarot system. Printed titles, numbering, suit names, missing cards, regional conventions, and visual differences must be preserved rather than silently replaced with Rider–Waite–Smith assumptions.

## Visual style

Fine Renaissance engraving, elaborate armor, weapons, vessels, furnaces, tools, banners, classical costume, inscriptions, dramatic single figures, and fully illustrated pips later hand-colored in the surviving complete set. The visual narratives are dense, idiosyncratic, and frequently unlike later tarot conventions.

Future card descriptions must begin with literal inspection of the relevant historical image: figures, posture, direction, objects, heraldry, inscriptions, color blocks, number and placement of suit emblems, printing irregularities, damage, restoration choices, and any difference between surviving copies.

## Divination philosophy

The deck may be read historically, visually, symbolically, comparatively, or divinationally, but those modes should not be confused.

1. **Observe the actual card.** Edition-specific visual evidence comes before memorized keywords.
2. **Preserve historical structure.** Do not renumber trumps, rename suits, invent missing originals, or import illustrated-pip scenes from another deck.
3. **Distinguish fact from interpretation.** Production history, surviving-card status, printed inscriptions, and later occult correspondences are different categories.
4. **Read contextually.** A card’s role changes with the question, position, neighboring cards, direction, repetition, and chosen historical or divinatory method.
5. **Treat later systems as optional overlays.** Golden Dawn, Waite–Smith, Etteilla, Marseille, astrological, Kabbalistic, and modern psychological correspondences should be named when used rather than presented as original to this edition.
6. **Preserve agency and evidence.** Tarot does not prove another person’s private thoughts, override consent, replace professional advice, or guarantee a future event.
7. **Synthesize rather than keyword-match.** Explain how the card’s visible and historical features support the reading.

## Numerology

Mato is unnumbered or treated as zero; named trumps run `I–XXI`. Suit numbers and courts form a complete 56-card Minor sequence, but later Golden Dawn or Waite–Smith numerology should be identified as comparative rather than original.

Formal numbers should be read first in the deck’s own sequence. Reductions, repeated numbers, elemental counts, or later esoteric correspondences may be used as secondary methods only when the reader identifies the system being applied.

## Suit language

Wands, Cups, Swords, and Coins are fully illustrated through human action rather than abstract emblem count alone. Court rank and suit remain important, but objects, occupation, machinery, gesture, inscription, and historical or alchemical context are primary.

Court rank, pip arrangement, and suit order are edition-specific evidence. Where pip cards are not scenically illustrated, the count, geometry, plant ornament, crossings, openings, and relation between emblems become especially important.

## Recurring deck motifs

Armor, shields, furnaces, fire, vessels, blades, standards, classical names, military posture, tools, sacrifice, labor, alchemical-looking operations, and the tension between heroic display and bodily strain recur throughout the pack.

Repetition across a spread—gestures, crowns, animals, celestial forms, architectural openings, vegetation, weapons, vessels, colors, directions, or suit-emblem arrangements—should influence interpretation when it is genuinely visible in this edition.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are optional. Historical packs were not always designed around the modern practice of mechanically assigning an opposite meaning to an inverted card.

When reversals are used, they may indicate blocked, delayed, inward, excessive, diminished, private, unstable, recovering, or differently oriented expression. The card’s actual inverted composition, the selected reading system, and surrounding cards should determine the interpretation.

## Historical source notes

The complete 78-card set is conventionally dated 1491 and is historically important as both a complete tarot and a fully illustrated suit sequence. The cards inspired later artists, including recognizable compositional borrowings in Pamela Colman Smith’s work, but the Sola Busca must not be described through those later meanings alone.

Dates and titles for historical decks are sometimes approximate or normalized across catalogues. Repository text should identify uncertainty rather than converting scholarly convention into false precision.

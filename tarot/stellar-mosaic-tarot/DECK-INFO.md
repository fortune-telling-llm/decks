# Stellar Mosaic Tarot

## Deck identity

**Deck name:** Stellar Mosaic Tarot  
**Deck family:** Tarot  
**Status:** In development  
**Planned structure:** 78 cards (22 Major Arcana + 56 Minor Arcana)  
**Primary reading style:** image-led intuitive divination supported by traditional tarot correspondences, symbolism, numerology, elemental associations, and repeating visual motifs.

## Core concept

Stellar Mosaic Tarot presents each card as a meeting point between the **cosmic** and the **human**. Celestial geometry, stars, halos, constellations, radiant light, mountains, rivers, plants, clouds, and other natural motifs are rendered as if they were assembled from stained glass and hand-laid mosaic.

The deck's central idea is:

> **The larger pattern becomes meaningful through the small pieces.**

A mosaic is made from fragments that only reveal their full picture when viewed together. This makes the visual style part of the divinatory system rather than decoration: a reader should be able to notice individual details, relationships between symbols, repeated numbers, colors, directions, objects, and environmental features, then connect those observations into a larger reading.

## Visual style

The deck uses a consistent visual language:

- **Mosaic / stained-glass construction:** figures, hands, clouds, landscapes, and celestial objects are visibly segmented into tile-like shapes.
- **Deep celestial backgrounds:** midnight blue, indigo, violet, and black create a night-sky field.
- **Luminous gold linework:** borders, circular geometry, stars, rays, and important objects use warm gold to suggest energy, fate, illumination, and connection.
- **Sacred geometry and celestial rings:** circular frames and radial structures suggest cycles, fields of possibility, alignment, and the relationship between the individual card and a larger order.
- **Elemental light:** each suit should express its element not only through objects but through atmosphere, motion, color, and texture.
- **Detailed landscapes:** mountains, rivers, flowers, clouds, paths, horizons, and terrain provide secondary symbols that can materially change a reading.
- **Strong central silhouettes:** cards remain readable at small size while still rewarding close inspection.
- **Black and gold frame:** a consistent ornate border makes the deck feel like one coherent object.
- **Number medallion at the top:** number symbolism remains visually available even before the reader looks at the title.
- **Card title at the bottom:** traditional card names are kept clear and readable.

The style is intentionally original and is **not designed to imitate a named living artist**.

## Divination philosophy

This deck is designed for a fortune-teller—human or AI—to read the **actual picture**, not merely retrieve a memorized tarot definition.

When interpreting a card, use this order:

1. **Observe what is literally present.** Identify people, hands, objects, animals, plants, landscape, celestial features, weather, light, colors, numbers, shapes, directions, gestures, distances, and relationships between objects.
2. **Notice emphasis.** Which symbol is largest, brightest, central, hidden, repeated, isolated, ascending, descending, blocked, flowing, blooming, burning, broken, etc.?
3. **Read deck-specific symbolism.** Use the meanings documented in the card's `.md` file and recurring motifs defined here.
4. **Connect traditional tarot correspondences.** Arcana, suit, element, number, and established tarot meanings provide the structural backbone.
5. **Look for cross-card patterns.** Repeated numbers, elements, colors, flowers, celestial forms, terrain, directions, or compositions across a spread should influence the interpretation.
6. **Apply the question.** A symbol can express itself differently in love, work, money, health/wellbeing, spirituality, a person's character, or the relationship between two people.
7. **Synthesize rather than keyword-match.** The final meaning should explain how the visible symbols interact in this specific card and spread.

The documented meaning is a strong reference, not a rule that overrides the actual spread.

## Numerology

Numbers are intentionally exposed in the artwork and should be read on multiple levels.

- **1 / Ace:** origin, singularity, emergence, initiative, raw potential, self-directed force, the first visible expression of an element.
- Repeated numbers inside a spread can amplify a theme.
- Counts of secondary objects may matter when visually clear and intentional enough to notice. These should be described as **supporting signals**, not automatically treated as equal to the card's formal number.
- Reductions and patterns (for example, multiple 3s, mirrored numbers, sequences, or a concentration of odd/even numbers) may be considered when the spread makes them relevant.

## Suit language

### Wands — Fire

Wands represent **will, ignition, creativity, desire, vitality, courage, initiative, ambition, sexuality/chemistry, entrepreneurship, inspiration, and the force that makes something begin to move**.

Visual tendencies: flame, gold and orange light, radiance, upward motion, sparks, dry or sunlit terrain, stars that resemble ignition points, and strong vertical forms.

### Cups — Water

Cups will emphasize emotion, receptivity, intuition, relationships, memory, empathy, imagination, and spiritual/psychic sensitivity.

Visual tendencies: water, moonlight, reflections, mist, vessels, tides, flowing curves, silver-blue light, and emotionally expressive environments.

### Swords — Air

Swords will emphasize thought, truth, decisions, communication, conflict, analysis, boundaries, perception, and mental pressure.

Visual tendencies: wind, open sky, sharp geometry, feathers, clouds, directional movement, pale light, and high contrast.

### Pentacles — Earth

Pentacles will emphasize material reality, resources, money, work, health of routines, the body, skill, stability, cultivation, and long-term results.

Visual tendencies: stone, soil, roots, forests, gardens, architecture, minerals, green/gold earth tones, and dense tactile detail.

## Recurring deck motifs

These motifs may recur across cards. Their meaning must still be adapted to the specific card.

- **Celestial ring / halo:** a field of possibility, cosmic order, cycles, alignment, or a force larger than the individual.
- **Starburst:** activation, revelation, a point of concentrated energy, sudden clarity, or an event that cannot remain dormant.
- **Mosaic body or hand:** human agency assembled from many experiences; the individual acting as one piece within a larger pattern.
- **Cloud threshold:** emergence from the unseen, uncertainty becoming visible, a message/force crossing into ordinary reality.
- **Mountain:** aspiration, difficulty, distance, endurance, a high objective, or something that must be climbed.
- **River / winding water:** the path as process; movement through changing terrain; continuity, adaptation, and a route that is real even when its final destination is not yet visible.
- **Rose:** passion made tangible; beauty, desire, affection, creative flowering, and the vulnerability that accompanies something valued.
- **Gold:** living energy, illumination, value, active spiritual force, and emphasis.
- **Deep blue / indigo:** mystery, the unconscious, distance, night, the unseen pattern, and contemplative depth.
- **Circular geometry:** cycles, wholeness, repetition, orbit, containment, and the relationship between center and surrounding forces.

## Reading reversals

Reversals are supported, but they should **not** be interpreted by mechanically negating the upright meaning.

A reversed card can indicate:

- blocked or delayed expression;
- inward/internalized expression;
- excess or dysregulation of the card's energy;
- misdirection;
- a private rather than public process;
- recovery from the difficult side of the card;
- or a need to examine the card's imagery from a different directional relationship.

The question and surrounding cards determine which expression is most plausible.

## File convention

- a `.jpg` containing the artwork;
- a matching `.md` containing a literal visual inventory plus detailed symbolic and divinatory interpretation.

The card markdown should be detailed enough that a model can reason about the image even when direct image access is unavailable, while direct image inspection remains preferred whenever possible.
